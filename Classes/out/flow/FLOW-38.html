1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What do you enjoy doing in your free time, and what do you hope to do next year?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: I enjoy cooking, and I hope to learn Italian. Let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Verb + -ing: enjoy, avoid, mind, suggest, finish. Verb + to-infinitive: decide, hope, promise, refuse, plan.

Examples

  • I enjoy reading, and I avoid watching the news before bed.
  • I have decided to change jobs, and I hope to start soon.
  • She refused to sign the contract, but she promised to reply tomorrow.

Execution method: Show two lists of verbs, one with -ing and one with to-infinitive. Ask guiding questions: Does the verb take an action or a plan? Can you say enjoy to do? Then ask the student to finish three sentences: I enjoy ..., I decided ..., I suggest ....

Check question: Do we say I enjoy to read or I enjoy reading?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: Sara enjoys walking to work, and she avoids taking the car in the rain. She has decided to save money, so she hopes to move closer to the office. Her friend suggested buying a bike, and Sara promised to think about it.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does Sara enjoy? (walking to work). What does she avoid? (taking the car in the rain). What has she decided to do? (save money). What did she promise? (to think about buying a bike).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is Sara positive or unsure about the bike? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows her uncertainty, such as promised to think about it.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Plans and habits: the student tells the tutor about two things they enjoy or avoid doing, and two things they have decided or hope to do. The tutor asks one question: What do you plan to do first?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I enjoy ...
  • I decided to ...
  • I hope to ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student finish each sentence, and note verb-pattern errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I enjoy to swim in the sea.
  • I decided going to change my job.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
