1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask an open question: Have you ever done something unusual? Give a 15-second example: I have climbed a mountain. It was tiring but amazing.

Tutor action: Let the student speak for 1 to 2 minutes without interruption. Note two target structures for the wrap-up.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Subject + have / has + past participle (ever, never, already, yet)

Examples

  • I have visited Spain twice.
  • Have you ever tried sushi?
  • She has never flown on a plane.

Execution method: Show the three sentences in context and ask guiding questions: When did the action happen? Do we know the exact time? Then ask the student to state the rule in their own words.

Check question: Do we know the exact time when the action happened, or does it matter only that it happened in life?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Listen to a short podcast extract or read this text twice at normal speed: My grandfather has worked as a fisherman for forty years. He has seen many storms, but he says he has never stopped loving the sea. Last year he finally retired, and he has already started teaching his grandchildren to sail.

Direct fact check

Ask: How long has the grandfather worked as a fisherman? (forty years). What has he already started doing? (teaching grandchildren to sail).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: What is the main idea of the text? Ask the student to infer the grandfather's attitude to the sea and point to the phrase that shows it.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Life experience discussion: the student tells the tutor about three experiences they have had (travel, food, work, hobbies). The tutor listens without interrupting and asks one follow-up question: What was the best part?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I have never ...
  • I have already ...
  • Have you ever ...?

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student speak for at least 60 to 65 percent of the class and note mistakes silently for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I have visit Spain twice.
  • Have you ever eat sushi?

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
