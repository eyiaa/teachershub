1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: Have you ever arrived somewhere and found that something had already happened or changed?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: When I got to the cinema, the film had already started. Let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Past perfect (had + past participle) for the earlier event; past simple for the later event: When I arrived, the train had already left.

Examples

  • When I arrived at the station, the train had already left.
  • She had finished her homework before her friends called.
  • After they had eaten, they went for a walk.

Execution method: Show a timeline with two past events and label the earlier one. Ask guiding questions: Which event happened first? Which verb form shows the earlier event? Then ask the student to say what had happened before they arrived at work or school today.

Check question: In When I arrived, the train had already left, which event happened first?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: When Maya got to the airport, her flight had already boarded. She had booked the wrong time. The staff had tried to call her, but her phone had run out of battery.

Direct fact check

Ask: What had already happened when Maya arrived? (her flight had boarded). What had she booked? (the wrong time). Why could the staff not reach her? (her phone had run out of battery).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is Maya calm or stressed about the situation? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows her problem, such as had booked the wrong time.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Missed moment: the student describes a time they arrived too late for something, using past perfect for what had already happened. The tutor asks one question: What did you do next?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • When I arrived, ... had already ...
  • I had forgotten ...
  • By the time I got there, ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student finish the sequence, and keep tense corrections for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • When I arrived, the train already left.
  • When I arrived, the shop closed.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
