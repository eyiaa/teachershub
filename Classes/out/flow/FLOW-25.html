1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: When did you last visit a doctor or pharmacist, and what did you need?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: I went to the pharmacy for some cold medicine. Let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Doctor visit: How long have you had it? / Where does it hurt? / You should ... / You mustn't ... / Take this twice a day.

Examples

  • How long have you had the cough?
  • Where does it hurt, exactly?
  • You should rest, and you mustn't go to work until the fever goes down.

Execution method: Show the doctor questions and the advice sentences. Ask guiding questions: Who asks the questions? Which modal gives strong advice? Then ask the student to write one question a doctor might ask about a headache.

Check question: In You mustn't go to work, is the advice strong or optional?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this advice column reply twice at a steady pace: Dear Sally, your back pain may be from sitting too long. You should stretch every hour, and you don't have to stop working, but you mustn't lift heavy boxes for a week. If it continues, you should see a doctor.

Direct fact check

Ask: What may be causing the back pain? (sitting too long). What should Sally do every hour? (stretch). What must she not do for a week? (lift heavy boxes).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the writer strict or friendly? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the writer is reassuring, such as you don't have to stop working.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Doctor role-play: the student is a patient describing a symptom to the tutor, who plays the doctor. The doctor asks two questions and gives two pieces of advice. Then the student writes two sentences of advice to a friend with the same problem.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • It has been ... for ...
  • You should ...
  • You mustn't ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the patient describe the symptom fully, and note errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • It hurts since two days.
  • You should to drink water and rest.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
