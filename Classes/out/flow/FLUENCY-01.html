1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What is something you find hard to explain in English?

Tutor action: Let the student speak for about one minute, without correcting their pauses.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Filler (well, actually, let me think, that's a good question) + short pause + main idea

Examples

  • Well, I think the main issue is cost.
  • That's a good question. Let me think for a moment.
  • Actually, I would say it depends on the team.

Execution method: Show a list of natural fillers with their jobs (buying time, softening, changing direction). Ask guiding questions: What is the speaker doing while they think? Which filler buys time without changing the meaning? Then ask the student to use one filler in a short answer.

Check question: Does That's a good question give you time to think, or does it answer the question?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Listen to or read this answer twice at a steady pace: Well, that's a good question. Let me think. Actually, I'd say the biggest challenge is communication, because people don't always say what they mean.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the speaker say first? (that's a good question). What is the biggest challenge? (communication). Why? (people do not always say what they mean).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the speaker sound hesitant or thoughtful? Ask the student to point to the filler that shows they are thinking, such as let me think.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Thinking time: the tutor asks one open question about a familiar topic (a hobby, a favourite place, a recent change). The student answers using at least two fillers, aiming for short natural pauses rather than silence.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Well, I think ...
  • Let me think ...
  • Actually, I would say ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student pause and recover without help. Praise natural fillers, and do not correct hesitation during the answer.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Eh, I think, eh, the city is nice, eh.
  • I am thinking, I am thinking, the answer is yes.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
