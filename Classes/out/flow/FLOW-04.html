1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What is one rule at your workplace or school? Give a 15-second example: You have to wear a badge at work.

Tutor action: Let the student speak freely for 1 to 2 minutes. Note modal verbs for the wrap-up.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Subject + should (advice) / must (strong obligation) / have to (external rule) + base verb

Examples

  • You should drink more water.
  • You must lock the door when you leave.
  • We have to wear uniforms at this school.

Execution method: Show the three examples. Ask guiding questions: Who makes the rule? Is it a suggestion or a requirement? Then ask the student to decide which modal fits each situation.

Check question: If a rule comes from the company, do we use must or have to?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice: New employees should read the safety guide before they start. They have to wear helmets on the factory floor, and they must never leave the machines running when no one is there.

Direct fact check

Ask: What should new employees read? (the safety guide). What do they have to wear? (helmets). What must they never do? (leave the machines running).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the tone of the text friendly or strict? Ask the student to point to the word that shows the level of obligation, such as must.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Advice column: the student reads a short problem card (a friend is always late; a neighbour plays loud music) and gives advice to the tutor using should, must, or have to. The tutor responds with one question.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • You should ...
  • You must ...
  • You have to ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student finish the advice before responding, and keep the wrap-up for modal corrections.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • You should to drink more water.
  • You must to wear a helmet.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
