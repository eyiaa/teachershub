1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What did your last manager or teacher tell you to do?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: My manager told me to finish the report by Friday. Let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Statement: said (that) + back-shifted tense (is → was; will → would). Question: asked + if / whether, or question word + subject + verb. Command: told / asked + object + to + verb

Examples

  • She said, I am tired. → She said she was tired.
  • He asked, Where do you live? → He asked me where I lived.
  • The teacher told us to wait outside.

Execution method: Show direct speech on the left and reported speech on the right for each type. Ask guiding questions: What changed in the tense? Did the question word order change? Then ask the student to report one thing a friend said to them this week.

Check question: In He asked me where I lived, do we keep the question word order or change it?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: Yesterday my manager said the meeting would start late. She asked us whether we had read the report. She told us to bring our laptops tomorrow.

Direct fact check

Ask: When would the meeting start? (late). What did the manager ask? (whether they had read the report). What did she tell them to bring? (their laptops).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the manager's tone friendly or firm? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the instruction, such as told us to bring.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Message relay: the tutor says a short statement, a question, and a command. The student reports each one to a colleague. Then the student reports one thing the tutor said earlier in the lesson.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • He said that ...
  • She asked me whether ...
  • They told us to ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student finish each report, and note tense back-shift errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • She said that she is tired.
  • He asked me where do I live.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
