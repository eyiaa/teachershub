1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: If you met someone new at a conference, what would you tell them about yourself first?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: I work in marketing and I live with my family in a small town. Let the student speak freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Introduction: I'm ... / I work as ... / I live with ... / I have got ... (family) / I'm originally from ... / I'm keen on ... (interests)

Examples

  • I'm Carlos, and I work as an engineer in Madrid.
  • I've got two younger sisters, and we all get on very well.
  • I'm originally from Peru, but I've lived here for five years.

Execution method: Show the introduction structure in three parts: name and job, family, and background. Ask guiding questions: What information comes first in an introduction? Which tense do we use for how long you have lived somewhere? Then ask the student to build their own introduction in three sentences.

Check question: In I have lived here for five years, is the action finished or still continuing?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this email twice at a steady pace: Hello Anna, I'm Sam, and I work as a designer in a small agency. I've got a dog called Max, and I'm really keen on hiking. I'm originally from Ireland, but I've lived in Berlin since 2019. Best wishes, Sam.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is Sam's job? (designer). What does Sam have? (a dog called Max). Where is Sam originally from? (Ireland). Since when has he lived in Berlin? (2019).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the email formal or friendly? Ask the student to point to the phrase that makes it friendly, such as Hello Anna or really keen on.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: New contact role-play: the tutor plays a new colleague at a welcome lunch. The student introduces themselves, talks about their family, and asks the tutor two questions about their life.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I'm ... and I work as ...
  • I've got ...
  • Where are you from originally?

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student lead the introduction, and note errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I am working as a designer since two years.
  • I have a dog, it is called Max.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
