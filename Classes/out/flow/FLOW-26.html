1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a photo of a crowded train station on a Sunday. Ask: Why do you think there are so many people here?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: It must be a holiday weekend. Let the student guess freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: must be (very sure); might or could be (possible); can't be (very sure it is not). Ability: can / could / be able to; permission: be allowed to

Examples

  • The lights are on, so she must be home.
  • That might be the bus. It could be late again.
  • He can't be at work, because his car is outside. He is able to speak three languages, and he is allowed to leave early on Fridays.

Execution method: Show a certainty scale: can't be, might be, must be. Ask guiding questions: How sure is the speaker? Is the situation present or past? Then ask the student to make one guess about a person they can see in the photo.

Check question: Does might be show more certainty or less certainty than must be?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: The office is empty, so everyone must be at the meeting. Anna could be in the training room, or she might be on a call. She can't be at lunch, because her bag is still here.

Direct fact check

Ask: Where must everyone be? (at the meeting). Where could Anna be? (in the training room, or on a call). Where can't she be? (at lunch).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: How sure is the writer about where Anna is? Ask the student to point to the word that shows the writer is not certain, such as could or might.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Guess the situation: the tutor describes a scene (a parked car with its engine running, a closed shop with the lights on). The student makes three guesses using must be, might be, and can't be, and says what ability or permission a person might have.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • It must be ...
  • It might be ...
  • It can't be ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student make each guess fully, and challenge a guess with one question: How sure are you?

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • She must to be at home.
  • He can't to speak French.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
