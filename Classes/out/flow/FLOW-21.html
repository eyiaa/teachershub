1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What did you study, or what would you like to study, and why?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: I studied business at university, but I would like to study design next. Let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Phrasal verbs: apply for (a job or course) / turn down (refuse an offer) / give up (stop trying); workplace nouns: a CV, a deadline, a promotion, a shift

Examples

  • I applied for a job at the design studio last week.
  • She turned down the offer because the salary was too low.
  • Don't give up. You can still apply for the next course.

Execution method: Show each phrasal verb with its meaning and an example. Ask guiding questions: Does apply for need a noun after it? Is turn down a refusal or an acceptance? Then ask the student to use two of the phrasal verbs to describe their own study or work.

Check question: If someone turns down an offer, do they accept it or refuse it?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: Jake applied for a job in an online shop, but he turned down the first offer because the shift was too early. He nearly gave up, but then he found a better position with a flexible timetable.

Direct fact check

Ask: What did Jake apply for? (a job in an online shop). Why did he turn down the first offer? (the shift was too early). What did he almost do? (give up).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is Jake disappointed or hopeful at the end? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the ending, such as found a better position.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Career story: the student tells the tutor about a time they applied for something, turned something down, or nearly gave up. The tutor asks one question: What did you do next?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I applied for ...
  • I turned down ... because ...
  • I nearly gave up, but ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student finish the story, and note vocabulary errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I applied the job at the company.
  • I turned the offer down because it was too low pay.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
