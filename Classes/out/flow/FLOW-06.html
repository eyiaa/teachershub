1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What do you think the world will be like in twenty years? Give a 15-second example: I think cars will drive themselves.

Tutor action: Let the student speak freely for 1 to 2 minutes and note the forms they use.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Will + base verb (prediction or instant decision); am / is / are going to + base verb (plan or evidence now)

Examples

  • I think it will rain later.
  • Look at those clouds. It is going to rain.
  • I will call you back in a minute.

Execution method: Show the three examples. Ask guiding questions: Is there evidence now? Was the decision made before speaking? Let the student sort each example into prediction, plan, or instant decision.

Check question: In Look at those clouds, is the prediction based on evidence now or a decision made in the moment?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice: The company is going to open a new office next year because sales have grown. Many experts think the city will become a major business centre in the next decade.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is the company going to open? (a new office). Why? (sales have grown). What do experts think will happen? (the city will become a major business centre).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the writer certain about the business centre prediction? Ask the student to find the word that shows the level of certainty, such as think.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Prediction debate: the student makes two predictions about technology or the environment and gives a reason for each using because. The tutor offers one counter-prediction.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I think ... will ...
  • It is going to ... because ...
  • I will ... in a minute.

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student finish both predictions before responding, and keep corrections for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I think it going to rain.
  • Look at the clouds. It will rain.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
