1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a photo of a street in the rain with people running. Ask: What do you think was happening when this photo was taken?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: People were walking home when it suddenly started to rain. Let the student speculate for one to two minutes.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Past continuous (was / were + verb-ing) for the background or ongoing action; past simple for the main event that interrupts or follows. Sequencing: meanwhile, eventually, in the end.

Examples

  • I was walking home when it started to rain.
  • While the team was preparing the report, the manager called.
  • Eventually, the storm stopped, and in the end we got home safely.

Execution method: Show the example pairs with the background and the main event coloured differently. Ask guiding questions: Which action was already happening? Which action interrupted it? Then ask the student to say what they were doing when something happened to them last week.

Check question: In I was walking home when it started to rain, which action was already in progress?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: Last summer, Lucy was camping with her friends when a storm arrived. Meanwhile, the others were setting up the tent. Eventually the rain stopped, and in the end they had a wonderful evening by the fire.

Direct fact check

Ask: Who was Lucy with? (her friends). What arrived? (a storm). What were the others doing? (setting up the tent). How did the evening end? (wonderfully, by the fire).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the story end happily or unhappily? Ask the student to point to the sequencing word that shows the end of the story, such as in the end.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Story of a day: the student tells the tutor about a day they remember, using past continuous for the background and past simple for at least two main events. The tutor asks: And then what happened?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I was ... when ...
  • Meanwhile, ...
  • In the end, ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student finish the story, then note tense errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I walked home when it was starting to rain.
  • While we were eating, the phone was ringing.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
