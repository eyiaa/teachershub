1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What is one thing you have lost recently? Give a 15-second example: I have lost my keys, so I am late. Last week I lost my wallet.

Tutor action: Let the student speak freely for 1 to 2 minutes. Record two tense choices for later discussion.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Present perfect (have / has + past participle) for result now; past simple (verb + ed or irregular) for a finished time

Examples

  • I have lost my keys. (I cannot open the door now.)
  • I lost my keys yesterday. (The event is finished.)
  • She has finished the report. She finished it at noon.

Execution method: Show the two example pairs side by side. Ask guiding questions: Is there a finished time word? Does the event affect now? Let the student match each sentence to the rule.

Check question: Which sentence has a finished time word, and which one connects to the present?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at normal speed: Ana has moved to a new flat. She moved there last month. Since then she has met many neighbours, and she has finally found a good bakery nearby.

Direct fact check

Ask: When did Ana move? (last month). What has she met? (many neighbours). What has she found? (a good bakery).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: How does Ana feel about her new home? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the main idea, such as finally found a good bakery.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Story and result: the student tells the tutor about a recent event (a lost item, a finished project, a visit) using both tenses. The tutor asks: What happened next? Has it changed anything now?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I have ... so ...
  • I ... last ...
  • It has already ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student choose the tense first, and keep the wrap-up for corrections.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I have lost my keys yesterday.
  • She has finished it at noon.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
