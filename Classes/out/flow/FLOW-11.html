1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What do you usually do on weekday mornings? Then ask: What are you doing this month that is different?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: I usually walk to work, but this month I am taking the bus. Let the student answer freely for one to two minutes.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Routine or permanent state: subject + present simple (I work in a bank). Temporary situation: subject + am / is / are + verb-ing (I am working from home this week). Stative verbs (believe, belong, realise, suppose) do not usually take -ing.

Examples

  • I work in a hospital. I am working in a hospital this month, while my usual office is closed.
  • I believe you are right. (not: I am believing)
  • This book belongs to my sister. (not: is belonging)

Execution method: Show the two example pairs side by side. Ask guiding questions: Is this a routine or a temporary situation? Can we say I am believing? Then ask the student to make one routine sentence and one temporary sentence about their own life.

Check question: Would we say I am realising the answer, or I realise the answer, and why?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: Maria works as a teacher in Lisbon. This term she is covering a colleague's classes in a different school. She believes the new school suits her, and she realises that she enjoys the change.

Direct fact check

Ask: Where does Maria usually work? (as a teacher in Lisbon, the text implies). What is she doing this term? (covering a colleague's classes in a different school). What does she realise? (that she enjoys the change).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does Maria seem happy or unhappy with the change? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows her feeling, such as she enjoys the change.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Daily life and this month: the student describes a routine they have and one temporary situation in their life right now. The tutor asks one question: How long do you think this situation will last?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I usually ...
  • This week I am ...
  • I believe that ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student speak for at least 60 to 65 percent of the class and keep corrections for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I am believing that the plan is good.
  • I work this month in a different office.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
