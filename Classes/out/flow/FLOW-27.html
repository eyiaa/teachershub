1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: Describe the best hotel or place to stay that you have been to.

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: The hotel I liked was a small place that had a garden. Let the student describe it freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Defining relative clause: person (who / that), thing (which / that), place (where), possession (whose), with no commas: The man who checked us in was friendly.

Examples

  • The hotel which we booked was near the station.
  • This is the room where we stayed last year.
  • The guide whose tour we joined knew the city very well.

Execution method: Show each example with the noun and the defining clause highlighted. Ask guiding questions: Is the noun a person, a thing, a place, or a possession? Which pronoun fits? Then ask the student to define one place and one person from their trip, using a relative clause.

Check question: Do we use who or which to describe a person who checked us in?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: We stayed in a hotel that was close to the beach. The receptionist, who spoke four languages, gave us a room where we could see the sea. The taxi driver whose car was waiting took us to the station.

Direct fact check

Ask: Where did they stay? (a hotel close to the beach). What could the receptionist speak? (four languages). What could they see from the room? (the sea).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Did the trip sound comfortable or stressful? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the positive experience, such as where we could see the sea.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Trip description: the student describes a hotel or place they stayed at, using at least two defining relative clauses. The tutor asks one question: Would you go back there?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • The place where ...
  • The room which ...
  • The person who ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student finish the description, and note relative pronoun errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The hotel which we stayed it was nice.
  • The receptionist who he spoke four languages was kind.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
