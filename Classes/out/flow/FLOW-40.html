1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask an unprepared question on a familiar topic: What is a place in your town that you would recommend to a visitor, and why?

Tutor action: Let the student speak for one to two minutes without interruption. Note two strengths and two priorities for feedback.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Readiness rubric: range of structures (past, future, modals, conditionals); accuracy of verb forms; linking words (because, although, however); clear sequence in a short text

Examples

  • Range: If the bus is late, I will take a taxi.
  • Accuracy: I have lived here since 2019, and I have never regretted it.
  • Linking: Although it is busy, the market is worth visiting, because the food is fresh.

Execution method: Show the four rubric areas with one example each. Ask the student which area they feel strongest in, and which needs most work. Agree on one priority for the written task.

Check question: Which of the four rubric areas do you think needs the most practice before the next lesson?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Play a short role-play with the tutor as a hotel receptionist and the student as a guest with a problem with the room. Then the student writes a three-sentence message to the receptionist asking for a solution, using a linking word.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is the guest's problem? (the room). What does the guest want the receptionist to do? (solve the problem or move the guest). Check the written message for the request and one linking word.

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the message polite and clear? Check the message for a polite request, such as I would be grateful if.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Role-play and short opinion: the student responds as the hotel guest to the tutor's receptionist, then gives a short opinion in writing on whether hotels should offer quiet rooms. The student writes 60 to 80 words in the time remaining.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I would be grateful if ...
  • In my opinion, ...
  • Therefore, ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a neutral examiner. Do not interrupt the role-play. Note examples of each rubric area for the feedback, and share feedback only after the student has finished.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I want the room changed because I don't like it.
  • The hotel is good, I think, it is because of the people.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
