Contrast and Concession: However, Although, and Despite

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: Is there something you enjoy even though it is difficult? Give a 15-second example: I enjoy running, although it is hard in the heat.

Tutor action: Let the student speak freely for 1 to 2 minutes and note the linking words they use.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Although + clause (sentence), / Despite + noun or -ing, / The sentence. However, + new idea.

Examples

  • Although it was cold, we went swimming.
  • Despite the rain, the match continued.
  • The food was cheap. However, it was not very tasty.

Execution method: Show the three patterns with the contrast in each sentence highlighted. Ask guiding questions: What is the expected result? What is the surprising result? Let the student join two ideas with although.

Check question: After despite, do we use a full clause or a noun or -ing form?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice: Although the city has many parks, most people prefer to stay indoors in winter. However, the council is building new cycle paths, despite the budget cuts.

Direct fact check

Ask: What do most people prefer in winter? (to stay indoors). What is the council building? (new cycle paths). What is the problem with the budget? (budget cuts).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the writer positive or negative about the council's plan? Ask the student to point to the linking word that shows the contrast, such as however.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Balanced argument: the student gives two sides of a topic (for example, working from home) using although and however. The tutor responds with one counter-point and asks: What is your final opinion?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Although ..., ...
  • However, ...
  • Despite ..., ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student build the full contrast before responding, and keep linking-word errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Although it was cold, but we went swimming.
  • Despite it rained, we went out.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
