1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What is the funniest thing that has ever happened to you on a journey?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: Once I missed my train, so I took a taxi. Let the student speak for one to two minutes.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Beginning (set the scene: It was ... / Last year, ...); middle (the problem: Suddenly, ... / Then ...); end (the result: In the end, ... / Luckily, ...)

Examples

  • It was a cold morning, and I was rushing to catch the bus.
  • Suddenly, the driver stopped and said the route had changed.
  • In the end, I arrived late, but I met my best friend on the way.

Execution method: Show a short story divided into three parts. Ask guiding questions: Which part sets the scene? Which part has the problem? Which part gives the result? Then ask the student to match their warm-up story to the three parts.

Check question: Which sentence in the example story introduces the problem?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this story twice at a steady pace: It was a cold morning, and I was rushing to catch the bus. Suddenly, I realised I had left my phone at home. I went back, and in the end I caught the next bus. Luckily, the journey was calm, and I felt much better by lunchtime.

Direct fact check

Ask: What was the speaker rushing to do? (catch the bus). What did they realise? (they had left their phone at home). How did the story end? (they caught the next bus and felt better by lunchtime).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the story serious or light-hearted? Ask the student to point to the sentence that shows the speaker's feelings at the end, such as I felt much better.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Anecdote: the student tells the tutor a short personal story with a clear beginning, middle, and end. The tutor asks one question at the end: What did you learn from that?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • It was ..., and I ...
  • Suddenly, ...
  • In the end, ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student tell the full story, and keep corrections for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Suddenly I am realising I forgot my phone.
  • In the end I was arriving late and it was a good day.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
