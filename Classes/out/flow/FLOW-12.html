1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a photo of a friendly group at a party. Ask: What do you think these people are like?

Tutor action: Give a 15-second example: They look cheerful and easy to talk to. Let the student describe one person in the photo.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Adjective + preposition (afraid of, keen on, interested in); verb + preposition (get on with, depend on, look after)

Examples

  • I am afraid of spiders, but my brother is not.
  • She is very keen on cooking and interested in healthy food.
  • I get on well with my new colleagues.

Execution method: Show the example sentences with the preposition highlighted. Ask guiding questions: Which word comes after the adjective or verb? Is the next word a noun or -ing form? Then ask the student to finish three sentences: I am interested in ..., I get on with ..., I am afraid of ....

Check question: After interested, do we use in or on?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: Tom is a very easy-going person. He is keen on sport and interested in music. He gets on well with his brother, but he is afraid of heights, so he never climbs ladders.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is Tom keen on? (sport). What is he interested in? (music). Who does he get on well with? (his brother). What is he afraid of? (heights).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: How would you describe Tom's personality? Ask the student to point to the word that shows his character, such as easy-going.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Describe a friend: the student describes a friend's personality, interests, and one thing they are afraid of, using the target prepositions. The tutor asks: How long have you known each other?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I get on well with ...
  • She is very keen on ...
  • He is afraid of ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student finish each description, and note preposition errors silently for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • She is interested to music.
  • I get on very good with my friend.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
