1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: Have you ever watched a presentation that was hard to follow? What made it difficult?

Tutor action: Let the student answer freely, and note what they mention about speed or pauses.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Pause before a key point; stress the key word; slow down for numbers and names

Examples

  • We reduced costs by twenty percent. (pause) That is the key result.
  • The most important word here is trust.
  • Our target for next year is one million users.

Execution method: Model the same sentence twice: once flat, and once with a pause and stress. Ask guiding questions: Which word did the speaker stress? Where was the pause? Then ask the student to rehearse one key sentence with a deliberate pause.

Check question: Why does a pause before a key result help the audience?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this presentation extract twice at a steady pace: We spent two years on this project. (pause) And the result? Costs fell by a third. (pause) That single change is why we are here today.

Direct fact check

Ask: How long did the project take? (two years). How much did costs fall? (a third). What is the single change? (the cost reduction).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the speaker sound excited or calm? Ask the student to point to the phrase that builds the moment, such as And the result?

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Rehearse a key slide: the student practises the main sentence of a short talk with a pause and stress, then delivers the full sentence to the tutor. The tutor asks the student to repeat it with a different stress to change the meaning.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • The key point is ...
  • What matters most is ...
  • And the result?

Tutor mandate

Model deliberate pauses. Do not interrupt. Note where the student rushes, and give one specific pacing tip in the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • We reduced costs by twenty percent that is the key result.
  • The most important word is, trust, and we need trust.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
