1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What did you do last weekend?

Tutor action: Give a short example with one linking word, then let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Point + linking phrase + extra idea. Adding: on top of that. Emphasising: in fact. Returning: anyway.

Examples

  • The course was useful. On top of that, it was affordable.
  • I didn't expect to enjoy it. In fact, I loved it.
  • The weather was bad. Anyway, we still had a good time.

Execution method: Show the three linking phrases with their jobs. Ask guiding questions: Does this phrase add an idea, emphasise one, or return to the main point? Then ask the student to link two short sentences from their week.

Check question: Does anyway return to the main point, or introduce a contrast?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: The hotel was small. On top of that, the breakfast was excellent. In fact, it was the best part of the trip. Anyway, we would stay there again.

Direct fact check

Ask: What was small? (the hotel). What was excellent? (the breakfast). What was the best part of the trip? (the breakfast).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker positive overall? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the strongest opinion, such as the best part of the trip.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Two-minute story: the student describes a recent trip, meal, or event using at least three linking phrases. The tutor asks one question at the end to extend it.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • On top of that, ...
  • In fact, ...
  • Anyway, ...

Tutor mandate

Do not interrupt mid-sentence. Let the student link at least three ideas before responding, and note linking errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The hotel was small, and also the breakfast was very good, and in fact it was the best.
  • We would stay again, anyway, because, I think.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did I guide the student to discover the grammar rule instead of lecturing?

☐ Did I avoid interrupting mid-sentence during conversation?

☐ Is the student speaking for at least 60-65% of the class?

☐ Did we save error correction for the dedicated feedback stage?
