1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a photo of a football fan looking at a lost match. Ask: How would you describe how the fan feels?

Tutor action: Give a short example: The fan is bitterly disappointed. Let the student describe the feeling in one or two words.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Adverb + adjective collocation (bitterly disappointed, deeply concerned, strongly opposed); adjective + noun collocation (mitigating circumstances, sweeping changes); affixes (un- / in-, -ism, -ise, -ness)

Examples

  • She was bitterly disappointed by the decision.
  • The court considered the mitigating circumstances.
  • The reform will bring sweeping changes to the system.

Execution method: Show each collocation with a weaker alternative (very sad, bad reasons). Ask guiding questions: Which word is stronger? Does the adverb fit the adjective? Then ask the student to replace one weak adjective in their warm-up with a stronger collocation.

Check question: Is it more natural to say strongly opposed or strongly opposite in this context?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: Residents were deeply concerned about the plan. Many were strongly opposed to the sweeping changes, and their objections were not easily dismissed. The council cited mitigating circumstances for the delay.

Direct fact check

Ask: How were residents feeling? (deeply concerned). What were they opposed to? (the sweeping changes). What did the council cite? (mitigating circumstances for the delay).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer sympathise with the residents or with the council? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the attitude, such as not easily dismissed.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Describe a dispute: the student describes a disagreement at work or in a community, using at least three strong collocations. The tutor asks one follow-up question: What mitigating circumstances might explain it?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I was deeply ...
  • There are mitigating circumstances, including ...
  • The change will bring ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Let the student describe the situation fully, and share one stronger collocation live in the chat each time they use very or really.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • She was very disappointed by the decision.
  • The council had good excuses for the delay.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
