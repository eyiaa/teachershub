1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: What is the one decision you most want this audience to make today?

Tutor action: Let the student state the decision in one sentence, then ask what would make the audience hesitate.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Hook + thesis + three evidence points (with data language) + one anticipated objection + call to action (I would urge you to ... / Not only will this ..., but it will also ...)

Examples

  • I would urge you to approve the pilot this quarter.
  • Not only will this reduce costs, but it will also improve retention.
  • The objection that this is too expensive is fair, though the evidence suggests the return is faster than expected.

Execution method: Show the talk structure as a seven-minute sequence with time markers. Ask guiding questions: Where does the objection appear? Which sentence makes the call to action? Then ask the student to outline their own talk with the same structure.

Check question: Where in the talk should the anticipated objection appear, and why?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this peer feedback twice at native speed: Your opening was strong, and the data was presented clearly. However, you rushed the objection, so the audience did not feel it was taken seriously. I would urge you to slow down at that point. Not only would that help, but it would also make the close more persuasive.

Direct fact check

Ask: What was strong? (the opening and the clear data). What was rushed? (the objection). What does the reviewer recommend? (slowing down at that point). What would that also do? (make the close more persuasive).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the feedback constructive or harsh? Ask the student to point to the phrase that balances praise and criticism, such as However, you rushed the objection.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Seven-minute talk and debrief: the student delivers a recommendation to the tutor, who plays a board member. After the talk, the tutor asks one question about the call to action. The student then gives a two-minute self-debrief: one strength, one improvement, and one specific change for the next delivery.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I would urge you to ...
  • Not only will ..., but it will also ...
  • The next time I present this, I will ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a board member. Listen to the full talk without interrupting, ask one question about the call to action, and after the self-debrief give one observation the student did not mention.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I think you should approve it because it is good and it saves money.
  • The objection is not important, so I skip it.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
