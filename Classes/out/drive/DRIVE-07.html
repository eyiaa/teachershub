1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this sentence: The meeting was good and the team made a decision quickly. Ask: How would you describe this meeting more precisely?

Tutor action: Let the student suggest one improvement, then ask for a second, more precise word.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Basic word (make, good, big) → precise word (reach a consensus, productive, substantial) in a matching collocation

Examples

  • make a decision faster → speed up the decision process
  • a good result → a commendable outcome
  • a big problem → a considerable challenge

Execution method: Show three pairs on screen. Ask guiding questions: Is the new word more precise? Does it fit the same collocation? Let the student upgrade two sentences from their own week.

Check question: Which word is more precise in make a decision faster or speed up the decision process?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: The project went well, and the team reached a consensus quickly. The outcome was considerable, although the budget was a significant challenge at the start.

Direct fact check

Ask: How did the team reach agreement? (quickly, by consensus). What was a significant challenge? (the budget).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer describe the project as impressive or average? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the attitude, such as considerable.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Upgrade challenge: the student describes a recent project or event to the tutor, using at least five precise words instead of basic ones. The tutor responds naturally and asks one follow-up question.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • It was a considerable ...
  • We reached a consensus on ...
  • The outcome was commendable because ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Type a more precise word into the live chat each time the student uses a basic one, without interrupting their sentence.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The meeting was good and we made a decision fast.
  • It was a big problem for the team.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
