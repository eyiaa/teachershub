1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: A well-known shop closed suddenly last year. What do you think went wrong?

Tutor action: Let the student guess freely, then ask: What evidence would you need to be sure?

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Deduction from evidence: The shop must have ... / It might well have ... / Those signs point to ... / It all boils down to ...

Examples

  • The shop must have lost customers before the lease ended.
  • Rising rents might well have pushed the owners out.
  • It all boils down to cash flow, not to a single bad decision.

Execution method: Show a short case summary with three clues. Ask guiding questions: Which clue supports which deduction? Is the explanation certain or only likely? Then ask the student to make one deduction from the clues.

Check question: If the evidence is strong, do we use must have or might well have?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this case summary twice at native speed: The bakery closed in March. Sales had fallen for two years, the rent had doubled, and the owners had not updated their menu. Neighbours say the decision came suddenly, though the signs had been there for some time.

Direct fact check

Ask: When did the bakery close? (March). What had happened to sales? (they had fallen for two years). What had doubled? (the rent).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer blame the owners or treat the closure as mostly unavoidable? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the view, such as the signs had been there.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Case analysis: the student presents a reconstruction of the bakery's failure to the tutor, using at least two deductions and one abstract phrasal verb. The tutor, as an investor, asks: How certain are you of that?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • It must have ...
  • The evidence points to ...
  • It all boils down to ...

Tutor mandate

Act as an investor and coach. Challenge the student to match each claim to evidence, and share one stronger deduction phrase live in the chat while they speak.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The bakery can't have failed because of the rent.
  • The owners should not have been bad at business.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
