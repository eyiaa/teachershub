1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this sentence: Oh great, another meeting on Friday afternoon. Ask: How does the speaker feel? How do you know?

Tutor action: Let the student identify the tone, then ask what words signal the tone.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Idiom or phrase (on the fence, bite the bullet, the ball is in your court) + literal meaning vs. intended meaning

Examples

  • I am on the fence about the new offer. (I have not decided.)
  • We had to bite the bullet and cancel the trip. (We accepted an unpleasant choice.)
  • The ball is in your court now. (The next decision is yours.)

Execution method: Show each idiom with its literal and intended meaning. Ask guiding questions: Would the literal meaning make sense here? Let the student guess the meaning of a new idiom from context.

Check question: If someone says the ball is in your court, is the decision yours or theirs?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Play a 45-second clip of a sarcastic conversation, or read this text twice at native speed: Sure, let's just add another deadline. It is not like the team is already stretched thin. Honestly, I am on the fence about whether we can deliver.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is being added? (another deadline). Is the team already stretched? (yes, thin).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the first speaker serious or sarcastic? Ask the student to point to the words that show the tone, such as Sure and not like.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Office scenario roleplay: the tutor plays a manager who pushes an unreasonable deadline with sarcasm. The student replies diplomatically using at least two idioms and a tone that matches the situation.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I am on the fence about ...
  • We may have to bite the bullet ...
  • I think the ball is in ... court.

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Challenge the student to match the tone and avoid translating idioms from their native language. Share one better idiom live in the chat while they speak.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I am in the fence about the offer.
  • We will bite the bullet and it is not good.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
