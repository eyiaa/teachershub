1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this statement: Remote work is good for productivity. Ask: Do you agree? Give a view in thirty seconds.

Tutor action: Let the student speak for about thirty seconds, then offer a counter-point and listen for their first discourse marker.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Point + marker signalling its role: However (contrast) / Moreover (addition) / Nevertheless (concession) / That said (qualification)

Examples

  • The plan is ambitious. However, the budget does not support it yet.
  • The results were strong. Moreover, customer feedback was very positive.
  • The risks are real. Nevertheless, I believe the project is worth the investment.

Execution method: Show each marker with its role. Ask guiding questions: Does this marker add, contrast, or concede? Then ask the student to take their warm-up view and add one contrast and one addition with suitable markers.

Check question: Does nevertheless introduce agreement or a concession?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this presentation extract twice at native speed: The pilot programme was successful in most departments. However, the finance team reported delays. Moreover, two managers argued that the training was too short. That said, the overall results justify a wider rollout.

Direct fact check

Ask: How successful was the pilot? (in most departments). What did the finance team report? (delays). What did two managers argue? (the training was too short). What is the conclusion? (a wider rollout is justified).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker for or against the wider rollout? Ask the student to point to the marker that shows the concluding judgement, such as That said.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Balanced view: the student takes a side on a workplace change (flexible hours, open-plan offices, a new software system) and speaks for about two minutes. The tutor argues the other side, and the student replies with at least two markers: one concession and one counter-point.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • However, ...
  • Moreover, ...
  • That said, ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a discussion partner who disagrees. Let the student finish each contribution, and share one more precise discourse marker in the chat when the student repeats however or but.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The idea is good, but but the budget is small.
  • It is risky, and also it is good, and also the people like it.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
