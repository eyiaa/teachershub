1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: What is one decision you would change if you could go back? Ask: What would you do differently?

Tutor action: Challenge the student to explain the result of the change, not just the change itself.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: If + past perfect, would have + past participle (a past decision and its imagined result)

Examples

  • If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.
  • She would have been happier if she had taken the job.
  • If they had left earlier, they would not have missed the train.

Execution method: Show a past situation with its imagined result. Ask guiding questions: Did this happen? Can we change it now? Let the student write one regret and its result aloud.

Check question: Can we change a past result using the third conditional, or does it only describe what might have happened?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: Many entrepreneurs say they would have started their companies earlier if they had trusted their own judgement. Looking back, one founder admitted that if she had asked for advice sooner, she would have avoided two costly mistakes.

Direct fact check

Ask: What would entrepreneurs have done earlier? (started their companies). What would the founder have avoided? (two costly mistakes).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the founder bitter or reflective about the past? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the tone, such as looking back.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Life reflection: the student describes two past decisions and their results to the tutor using the third conditional. The tutor asks one probing question each time: What did you learn from that?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • If I had ..., I would have ...
  • Looking back, I ...
  • I wish I had ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Challenge the student to explain what they learned, and share one more natural phrase live in the chat while they speak.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • If I studied harder, I would passed the exam.
  • If she takes the job, she would be happier.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
