1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a painting with a dark sky over a small village. Ask: What mood does the artist create, and how?

Tutor action: Let the student describe the mood, then ask them to point to one visual detail that creates it.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Symbolism (an object standing for an idea) / movement (a shared style in a period) / cultural appropriation (borrowing from a culture without understanding or credit) / to subvert (to challenge a convention)

Examples

  • The dark sky is a symbol of uncertainty in the village.
  • The painting belongs to the late nineteenth-century realist movement.
  • Critics debated whether the designer's use of the pattern was cultural appropriation.

Execution method: Show each term with an example from art or media. Ask guiding questions: Does the object stand for an idea? Is the borrowing respectful or not? Then ask the student to describe one symbol they see in the warm-up painting.

Check question: Does subvert mean to follow a convention or to challenge it?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this review twice at native speed: The director's use of red throughout the film works as a symbol of danger, yet it also subverts the usual association with passion. Critics have debated whether the film's borrowing from folk traditions amounts to cultural appropriation.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the red symbolise? (danger). What does the film subvert? (the usual association with passion). What have critics debated? (whether the borrowing is cultural appropriation).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the reviewer approve of the director's choices? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows balance, such as yet it also subverts.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Critical review: the student gives a short critical review of a film, painting, or advert to the tutor, using at least three target terms and one balanced judgement. The tutor asks: What would a critic who disagrees say?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • The symbolism works because ...
  • This subverts the convention of ...
  • A critic might argue that ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a critic who disagrees. Challenge the student to support each judgement with evidence, and share one more precise critical term live in the chat when they use a general word such as good.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The painting is very good because it has a lot of colours.
  • The film does a thing with red that is different.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
