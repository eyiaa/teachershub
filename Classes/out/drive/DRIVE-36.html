1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: Does more screen time cause lower sleep quality, or are the two simply linked?

Tutor action: Let the student answer, then ask whether the evidence proves cause or only shows a link.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: X triggers Y (direct cause, often sudden) / Y stems from X (origin) / X exacerbates Y (makes worse) / X correlates with Y (association, not proof of cause)

Examples

  • The sudden price rise triggered a wave of protests.
  • Many health problems stem from poor sleep and stress.
  • Long working hours correlate with lower job satisfaction, but they do not prove cause.

Execution method: Show each verb with a short diagram of cause and effect. Ask guiding questions: Is the link proven or only observed? Is the effect sudden or gradual? Then ask the student to describe one cause and effect from their experience using one of the verbs.

Check question: Does correlate with prove that one thing causes the other?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: The study found that air pollution correlates with higher hospital admissions. Researchers believe the pollution may exacerbate existing conditions, although they stress that the findings do not prove cause.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does air pollution correlate with? (higher hospital admissions). What may pollution exacerbate? (existing conditions). What do the researchers stress? (the findings do not prove cause).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the writer certain that pollution causes the admissions? Ask the student to point to the phrase that limits the claim, such as do not prove cause.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Cause analysis: the student analyses a local problem (traffic, rising prices, a school issue) for the tutor, identifying one trigger, one underlying origin, and one correlation. The tutor asks: How do you know that is cause and not just association?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • The trigger was ...
  • This stems from ...
  • This correlates with ..., but ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a sceptical analyst. Challenge the student to separate cause from correlation, and share one more precise causal verb live in the chat when they overuse cause.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Pollution causes the hospital, the study proves it.
  • The price rise made the protests happen because of the price.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
