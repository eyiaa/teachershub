1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this scenario: You are buying a used car, and the seller wants a higher price. Ask: What would you say first?

Tutor action: Let the student make an opening offer, then respond as the seller and ask one counter-question.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: I am willing to + verb, provided that / as long as + condition; I could + verb, on condition that + condition

Examples

  • I am willing to pay more, provided that the car has a full service record.
  • We can extend the deadline, as long as the quality remains the same.
  • I could lower the price on condition that you cover the transfer costs.

Execution method: Show the phrases with a short negotiation example. Ask guiding questions: What is offered? What is the condition? Let the student rephrase one of their own offers as a conditional offer.

Check question: In I can lower the price on condition that you pay the fee, which part is the condition?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: The supplier is prepared to reduce the price, as long as the order is placed before the end of the month. However, the buyer would only accept the discount on condition that delivery is included.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is the supplier prepared to reduce? (the price). When must the order be placed? (before the end of the month). What does the buyer require? (delivery included).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the buyer cooperative or firm? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the position, such as would only accept.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Real-time negotiation: the student negotiates a deal with the tutor (renting a flat, agreeing a project timeline, buying a product). Each offer must include a condition using provided that, as long as, or on condition that.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I am willing to ..., provided that ...
  • We can ..., as long as ...
  • On condition that ..., I could ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Respond with realistic counter-offers and share one stronger phrase live in the chat when the student makes a weak offer.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I will pay more if the car is good.
  • I can lower the price, on condition you pay.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
