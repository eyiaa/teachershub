1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: What would have happened if the internet had been invented fifty years earlier?

Tutor action: Let the student speculate for about one minute, then ask one follow-up: What would have been the biggest loss?

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Suppose + past or present scenario, ... / Were it to happen, ... / Had the board known, it would have ...

Examples

  • Suppose the treaty had failed. The region would have faced a very different future.
  • Were it to happen again, the company would need a clear response plan.
  • Had the board known about the risk, it would have delayed the launch.

Execution method: Show the three hypothetical frames with one scenario each. Ask guiding questions: Is the scenario real or imagined? What is the consequence? Let the student build one hypothetical about the warm-up question using suppose.

Check question: In Were it to happen, is the speaker describing something likely or unlikely?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: Historians often ask what would have happened had the crisis been averted. Suppose the bank had been allowed to fail in 1929. Many economists argue that the recovery would have come sooner, though others doubt it.

Direct fact check

Ask: What do historians ask? (what would have happened had the crisis been averted). What do many economists argue? (the recovery would have come sooner).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer accept the economists' view or stay neutral? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the doubt, such as though others doubt it.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Ethics debate: the student takes a position on a corporate dilemma (a company knowingly sells a product with a small safety risk). The tutor argues the opposite side. Each turn, the student must use one hypothetical frame.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Suppose ..., then ...
  • Had the company ..., it would have ...
  • Were it to ..., we would ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner and debate opponent. Challenge the student to defend the position, and share one stronger hypothetical phrase live in the chat while they speak.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Suppose the bank fails, the people would lose money.
  • If the board knew, they will stop the launch.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
