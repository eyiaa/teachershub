1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show this statement from a colleague: We should cut the training budget to fund the launch. Ask: How would you respond without simply saying no?

Tutor action: Let the student respond, then play the colleague and push back once.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Disagreement frame: I see your point, but ... / I would be reluctant to ... / I am prepared to ..., provided that ... / Could we find a middle ground?

Examples

  • I see your point, but cutting training could hurt retention.
  • I would be reluctant to cut training entirely.
  • I am prepared to reduce the budget, provided that the core courses continue.

Execution method: Show a blunt disagreement and its diplomatic version. Ask guiding questions: What does the speaker concede? What is the condition? Then ask the student to rewrite one blunt objection from the warm-up as a diplomatic disagreement with a concession.

Check question: In I am prepared to reduce the budget, provided that the core courses continue, which part is the condition?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this meeting transcript twice at native speed: Manager: I see your point about the launch, but the training budget is needed. Colleague: Fair enough. I would be reluctant to cut it entirely, though I am prepared to reduce it, provided that the safety courses remain.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the manager say the training budget is? (needed). What is the colleague reluctant to do? (cut it entirely). What is the colleague prepared to do? (reduce it, provided that the safety courses remain).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the colleague cooperative or resistant? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows cooperation, such as Fair enough.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Compromise talk: the student negotiates a budget or timeline disagreement with the tutor, making at least one concession and at least one conditional offer. The tutor asks: Could we find a middle ground?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I see your point, but ...
  • I am prepared to ..., provided that ...
  • Could we find a middle ground on ...?

Tutor mandate

Act as a colleague who pushes back. Respond to each concession and offer, and share one more diplomatic phrase live in the chat when the student disagrees bluntly.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • No, we cannot cut the budget.
  • I will agree if you do what I say.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
