1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show this short scenario: Two colleagues disagree in a meeting, and one leaves the room. Ask: What do you think happened, and why?

Tutor action: Let the student offer one explanation, then ask what other explanation is also possible.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Motive (why someone acts) / trigger (what sets off a reaction) / likely outcome (what is probable next) / Framing: It is probably motivated by ..., which may have triggered ...

Examples

  • The colleague's motive was probably a wish to protect her team.
  • The comment acted as a trigger for the walk-out.
  • The likely outcome is a tense working relationship unless they meet.

Execution method: Show the scenario with each part labelled: motive, trigger, outcome. Ask guiding questions: Is this a fact or an inference? What would change the analysis? Then ask the student to name one motive and one trigger in the warm-up scenario.

Check question: Is saying the motive was probably to protect her team a fact or an inference?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: In the dispute, the manager's rigid tone seems to have been the trigger, while the team's frustration probably stemmed from months of unclear priorities. Unless both sides clarify their goals, the working relationship is likely to deteriorate.

Direct fact check

Ask: What seems to have been the trigger? (the manager's rigid tone). What did the team's frustration probably stem from? (months of unclear priorities). What is likely to happen unless the sides clarify goals? (the relationship will deteriorate).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer blame one side? Ask the student to point to the hedge that shows the inference, such as seems to and probably.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Scenario analysis: the student analyses a scenario the tutor describes (a team member refuses a new process) by identifying motive, trigger, and likely outcome. The tutor, as the manager, asks: What would you do first?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • The motive is probably ...
  • This may have been triggered by ...
  • The likely outcome is ...

Tutor mandate

Act as the manager and a sceptical colleague. Challenge the student to separate fact from inference, and share one more precise hedge live in the chat when they state an inference as fact.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • He is angry because he is a bad person.
  • The team will be unhappy for ever, it will happen.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
