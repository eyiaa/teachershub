1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show this sentence on screen: The mayor's plan is a total disaster. Ask: Is this a fair critique? How would you describe the tone?

Tutor action: Let the student judge the sentence first, then ask one question about the evidence behind it.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Critique frame: The writer appears to ... / The argument rests on ... / The tone is somewhat ... / The evidence is selective

Examples

  • The writer appears to assume that all delays are the council's fault.
  • The argument rests on a single anecdote rather than on data.
  • The tone is somewhat dismissive, which weakens an otherwise sound point.

Execution method: Show the critique frame with a sample paragraph. Ask guiding questions: What evidence is offered? Which words show the writer's attitude? Then ask the student to name one loaded word in the sample text.

Check question: Does the phrase the argument rests on describe the evidence, or the writer's attitude?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this opinion extract twice at native speed: The council's transport plan is a disaster that no sensible person could support. Its authors ignore the commuters who will suffer most, and their claim that the buses will run on time rests on a single optimistic report.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the writer say the authors ignore? (the commuters who will suffer most). What does their claim rest on? (a single optimistic report).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the writer neutral or biased? Ask the student to point to two loaded phrases, such as a disaster and no sensible person.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Rewrite and defend: the tutor reads a blunt paragraph aloud. The student rewrites it as a balanced critique in about two minutes, then defends the choices to the tutor, who plays the editor and questions the evidence.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • The writer appears to ...
  • The argument rests on ...
  • Although the concern is valid, ...

Tutor mandate

Act as an editor and language coach. Challenge the student to justify each change, and share one more precise word or phrase live in the chat when they hesitate.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The plan is terrible and the writer is biased.
  • The writer lies about the buses.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
