1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this scenario: A friend is offered a job abroad but does not speak the language. Ask: What would you do in their position?

Tutor action: Let the student answer instantly. Challenge them with one follow-up: Why would you choose that over the other option?

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: If + past simple, would + base verb (unreal present or future situation)

Examples

  • If I spoke fluent French, I would apply for the job.
  • If I were you, I would take the course.
  • If she had more time, she would learn the piano.

Execution method: Show the pattern and two contrasting sentences: a real situation (If it rains, I will stay) and an unreal one (If it rained, I would stay). Ask the student to explain the difference and then give advice to a friend in the warm-up scenario.

Check question: Does the second conditional describe a real possibility or an unlikely one?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: If the government raised taxes on sugar, many people would probably change their diet. Critics argue that it would hurt low-income families the most, though supporters say the health benefits would be worth it.

Direct fact check

Ask: What would many people probably change? (their diet). Who do critics think would be hurt most? (low-income families).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer present both sides fairly? Ask the student to point to the word that shows the balance, such as though.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Advice clinic: the student advises the tutor on a difficult dilemma (a job offer, a moving decision, a conflict with a colleague). The student must use at least three second conditionals and explain the reasoning for each piece of advice.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • If I were in your position, I would ...
  • If I had ..., I would have ...
  • It would be better to ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner and language coach. Challenge the student to defend their advice, and share one stronger phrase live in the chat while they speak.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • If I was you, I will take the course.
  • If I have more time, I would learn piano.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
