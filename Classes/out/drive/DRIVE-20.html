1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a photo of a car with a dented door parked next to a wall. Ask: What might have happened here?

Tutor action: Give a short example: It might well have been a parking accident. Let the student speculate and ask how sure they are.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: must / might well / can't have + past participle (deduction about the past); should have / needn't have / didn't need to + past participle (criticism or regret)

Examples

  • The lights are off, so they must have left already.
  • The train might well have been delayed by the storm.
  • You needn't have brought food. We didn't need to bring anything.

Execution method: Show the deduction modals on a certainty scale from can't have (very unlikely) to must have (very likely). Ask guiding questions: Is the speaker sure? Did the action actually happen? Then ask the student to make one deduction about the warm-up photo.

Check question: Does needn't have mean the action happened and was unnecessary, or that it did not happen?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: The report was late, and the team might well have caught the error earlier. They should have checked the figures twice. Still, nobody can have expected the software to fail so badly.

Direct fact check

Ask: What was late? (the report). What should the team have done? (checked the figures twice). What could nobody have expected? (the software failing so badly).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the writer blaming the team or excusing it? Ask the student to point to the phrase that softens the criticism, such as Still.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Case review: the student and tutor review a recent project delay. The student must make two deductions about why it happened and one criticism of a past decision, using the past modals.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • We must have ...
  • We should have ...
  • We needn't have ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Let the student reason through each deduction, and share one more precise modal phrase live in the chat when they hesitate.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The report might have to be late.
  • We didn't need to check it, we should not have.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
