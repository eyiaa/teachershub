1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: Should a city invest in self-driving buses now, or wait for more evidence?

Tutor action: Let the student give a quick opening view, then ask them which expert they would ask first.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Speaking as an expert: From a scientific view, ... / Ethically, the question is ... / Economically, the real cost is ... / I would add that ...

Examples

  • From a scientific view, the safety data is still limited.
  • Ethically, the question is who is responsible when a bus makes a mistake.
  • Economically, the real cost is the loss of driver jobs in the short term.

Execution method: Show the three expert frames on screen. Ask guiding questions: What does each expert focus on? Which point could the others challenge? Then ask the student to choose one expert role and state its opening view in two sentences.

Check question: If you speak as the economist, which point would you focus on first?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this panel transcript twice at native speed: Scientist: The trial data is promising but limited. Ethicist: Limited data is one thing; unclear accountability is another. Economist: Whatever we decide, the cost of waiting will fall on the drivers.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the scientist say about the data? (promising but limited). What does the ethicist worry about? (unclear accountability). What does the economist say falls on drivers? (the cost of waiting).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Are the experts in agreement or disagreement? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows each expert's concern, such as Limited data is one thing.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Panel discussion: the student speaks as one expert and responds to the tutor, who speaks as the other two. The student must respond to at least one point made by another panellist.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • From my perspective, ...
  • I would challenge the point that ...
  • Building on that, ...

Tutor mandate

Act as the other two panellists and a moderator. Challenge the student to respond directly to a point, and share one more precise response phrase live in the chat when they ignore an argument.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I think the data is limited, and the cost is high, and the people will be unhappy.
  • I agree with the scientist, he is right.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
