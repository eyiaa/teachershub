1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show this statement on screen: This policy will fail. Ask: How could you say this more diplomatically?

Tutor action: Give a short example: It may struggle in its current form. Let the student respond and then challenge them to soften one more word.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Hedge + claim: It could be argued that X / Presumably, X / To a certain extent, X / X may well be Y

Examples

  • This is the best option. → It could be argued that this is the strongest option.
  • The results are wrong. → To a certain extent, the results may be inaccurate.
  • He is the owner, presumably. → Presumably, he is the owner, though I have not checked.

Execution method: Show each direct claim beside its hedged version. Ask guiding questions: Which version sounds less certain? Why would a speaker choose a hedge in a meeting? Then ask the student to hedge one of their own opinions.

Check question: Does presumably show strong certainty or a reasonable assumption?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: The new scheme is presumably intended to reduce costs, though it could be argued that the savings will be modest. To a certain extent, the critics have a point.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is the scheme intended to do? (reduce costs). What could be argued about the savings? (they will be modest).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer strongly support or doubt the scheme? Ask the student to point to the phrase that softens the criticism, such as to a certain extent.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Diplomatic feedback: the student gives feedback on a colleague's plan, with the tutor playing the colleague. The student must use at least three hedges and must end with one clear recommendation.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • It could be argued that ...
  • To a certain extent, ...
  • Presumably, ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Respond as a sensitive colleague who may push back, and share one more diplomatic phrase live in the chat while the student speaks.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • This is definitely the best option, I think maybe.
  • Presumably, it will fail for sure.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
