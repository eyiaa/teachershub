1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show this sentence on screen: The new manager is a breath of fresh air. Ask: What does this mean, and what is the image?

Tutor action: Let the student explain the meaning, then ask what kind of image the metaphor creates.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Conceptual metaphor (an idea understood through another domain): ARGUMENT IS WAR (build a case, shoot down an idea); CHANGE IS WEATHER (a sea of change, a storm of criticism)

Examples

  • She built a strong case for the new budget.
  • The proposal was shot down in the first meeting.
  • The company is in the middle of a sea of change.

Execution method: Show each example with its source domain (war, weather, building) and its target meaning. Ask guiding questions: What is the image? What is the real idea? Then ask the student to use one metaphor from the screen in a sentence about their work.

Check question: In shot down an idea, which source domain is the metaphor drawn from?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: The debate was a storm of criticism, but the chair kept her head above water. She built a careful case, and by the end the proposal had gained real momentum.

Direct fact check

Ask: What kind of criticism was there? (a storm of criticism). What did the chair do? (kept her head above water, and built a careful case). What did the proposal gain? (real momentum).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the writer's view of the chair positive or negative? Ask the student to point to the metaphor that shows the chair coped under pressure, such as kept her head above water.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Metaphor story: the student describes a recent work or school situation using at least two metaphors from the theory slide. The tutor responds with one metaphor of their own and asks: What does that image tell us about the situation?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • We are facing a sea of change ...
  • She built a strong case for ...
  • The idea was shot down because ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Let the student use metaphors naturally, and share one more fitting image live in the chat when they use a literal phrase where a metaphor would sound more natural.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The project was a big storm of the work.
  • She made a strong box of arguments.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
