1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: What matters most to you in a job offer, beyond the salary?

Tutor action: Let the student answer, then ask them to rank their priorities in order and explain the top one.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: I am willing to + verb, provided that + condition / I can accept + offer, as long as + condition / Would it be possible to + verb + if + condition?

Examples

  • I am willing to accept the salary, provided that the training budget is included.
  • I can accept a flexible schedule, as long as I have two fixed days in the office.
  • Would it be possible to review the salary in six months, if I meet the targets?

Execution method: Show each frame with a short negotiation example. Ask guiding questions: What is offered? What is the condition? Is the proposal firm or open to discussion? Then ask the student to rewrite one of their demands as a conditional offer.

Check question: In I am willing to accept the salary, provided that the training budget is included, which part is the condition?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this negotiation transcript twice at native speed: Candidate: I am willing to accept a salary slightly below my expectation, provided that the company covers my certification fees. Recruiter: We could consider that, as long as you commit to two years with us. Candidate: That seems fair, if we can review the arrangement after one year.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is the candidate willing to accept? (a salary slightly below expectation). What does the candidate want covered? (certification fees). What is the recruiter's condition? (a commitment to two years).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the tone cooperative or competitive? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows a willingness to compromise, such as That seems fair.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Offer negotiation: the tutor plays a recruiter who has offered a salary and a fixed working pattern. The student responds with at least two conditional offers, asks about one benefit, and proposes a review date. The tutor counters with a condition of their own, and the student closes with a summary of the terms.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I am willing to ..., provided that ...
  • I can accept ..., as long as ...
  • Would it be possible to ...?

Tutor mandate

Act as a recruiter who needs to balance budget and retention. Make realistic counter-offers, and share one stronger negotiation phrase in the chat when the student makes an unconditional demand.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I want the salary higher, or I will not accept the job.
  • I accept if you give me the flexible hours.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
