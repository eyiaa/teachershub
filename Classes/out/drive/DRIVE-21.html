1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: What is one problem your team has had to solve recently?

Tutor action: Let the student speak for about one minute. Note any phrasal verb they use for the wrap-up.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Phrasal verb with an abstract meaning: iron out (solve problems), phase out (stop gradually), boil down to (reduce to the main point), come up against (face an obstacle)

Examples

  • We need to iron out the details before the launch.
  • The company plans to phase out the old system next year.
  • The debate boils down to one question: who pays?

Execution method: Show each phrasal verb with its abstract meaning and a literal sense for contrast. Ask guiding questions: Is the meaning physical or abstract? What is being solved, stopped, or reduced? Then ask the student to use one of the verbs in a sentence about their warm-up problem.

Check question: In The debate boils down to one question, is the speaker describing something physical or a simplification?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: The merger talks have come up against serious opposition from shareholders. The two boards will need to iron out their differences, and the old management structure will be phased out gradually. In the end, it all boils down to trust.

Direct fact check

Ask: What have the merger talks come up against? (serious opposition). What will the boards need to iron out? (their differences). What will be phased out? (the old management structure).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer sound optimistic or cautious about the merger? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the main obstacle, such as come up against.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Project status: the student explains a current problem to the tutor using at least three of the four phrasal verbs. The tutor asks one question: What does it all boil down to?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • We need to iron out ...
  • We are phasing out ...
  • It all boils down to ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Let the student complete each explanation, and share one more natural abstract phrasal verb live in the chat when they paraphrase with a plain verb.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • We will end the old system slowly.
  • The problem is that we come against many people.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
