1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: Why do people often stick with a decision even when the evidence says it is wrong?

Tutor action: Let the student give one reason quickly, then ask them to name the mechanism behind it.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Cognitive bias (a systematic error in thinking) / motivation (the reason for acting) / emotional trigger (something that provokes a strong response) / to rationalise (to find reasons after the fact)

Examples

  • Confirmation bias leads people to seek evidence that supports what they already believe.
  • Her motivation came from a need for security rather than from status.
  • The criticism was an emotional trigger, and he responded defensively.

Execution method: Show each term with a plain definition and an example. Ask concept-checking questions: Is a bias conscious or automatic? Does rationalise happen before or after the decision? Then ask the student to describe one bias they have noticed in themselves or others.

Check question: Does rationalise describe a reason given before a decision or after it?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: Managers often rationalise a failed project by pointing to bad luck. Yet the research suggests that confirmation bias plays a larger role, because people tend to notice the evidence that supports their earlier choice.

Direct fact check

Ask: How do managers often explain a failed project? (bad luck). What bias does the research suggest is more important? (confirmation bias).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer accept the managers' explanation? Ask the student to point to the word that signals the disagreement, such as Yet.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Decision review: the student describes a decision they regret or one they are proud of, and explains which bias or motivation shaped it. The tutor asks one question: Would you make the same decision again?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I think my motivation was ...
  • In hindsight, I was influenced by ...
  • It may have been a case of ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Let the student explain the reasoning fully, and share one more precise term live in the chat when they use a general word such as feeling.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • He was angry because he has a bias.
  • I did the decision because I wanted to rationalise it.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
