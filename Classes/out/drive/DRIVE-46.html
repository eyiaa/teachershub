1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this unprepared question: Is it possible to be objective about the society you live in? Ask the student to answer in sixty seconds.

Tutor action: Start a one-minute timer. Let the student speak without interruption. Note two strengths and two priorities for feedback.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Readiness rubric: range of structures (conditionals, inversion, clefts, participles); precision of vocabulary (collocation, metaphor, abstract verbs); cohesion (discourse markers, referencing); formal register

Examples

  • Range: Had the data been clearer, we would have reached a firmer conclusion.
  • Precision: The proposal was shot down because it overlooked the risks.
  • Cohesion: Nonetheless, the committee approved the change, albeit with reservations.

Execution method: Show the four rubric areas with one example each. Ask the student which area they feel strongest in, and which needs the most work. Agree on one priority for the summary task.

Check question: Which of the four rubric areas do you think needs the most practice before the next lesson?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text once at native speed, then again at normal speed: The city's new housing policy aims to increase supply, yet many residents argue that it will mainly benefit developers. Whether the policy succeeds will depend on how well the council enforces its conditions and how honestly it reports the results.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the housing policy aim to do? (increase supply). Who do many residents think will benefit? (developers). What will decide success? (enforcement of conditions and honest reporting).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask the student to write a two-sentence formal summary of the text, without copying phrases. Check for register, accuracy, and a neutral tone.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Abstract discussion: the student speaks for two minutes on an unprepared abstract question chosen by the tutor, then debates the tutor for about six minutes. The tutor finishes by asking the student to summarise their own strongest point.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • On reflection, ...
  • I would qualify that by saying ...
  • The strongest point I would make is ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a neutral examiner. Do not interrupt the two-minute turn. Note examples of each rubric area for the feedback, and share feedback only after the student has finished.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The policy is good for everyone, and the people will be happy, I think.
  • The developers will benefit because they are the people who build.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
