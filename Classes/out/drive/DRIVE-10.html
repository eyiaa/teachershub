1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this topic: Should schools teach financial skills? Ask: Take thirty seconds to give your position.

Tutor action: Ask the student to state a position immediately, then challenge it with one counter-point.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Hook + thesis + two supporting points + concession + call to action

Examples

  • Imagine leaving school without knowing how to open a bank account. (hook)
  • My view is that financial education should be compulsory. (thesis)
  • Ultimately, the choice is clear, so let us act now. (call to action)

Execution method: Show the five-part frame on screen. Ask guiding questions: Which part grabs attention? Where is the concession? Let the student map their warm-up position onto the frame.

Check question: Which part of the speech asks the audience to act?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this speech extract twice at native speed: Imagine a world where every young person can manage their own money. Critics say schools are already overloaded, yet financial skills affect every decision we make, so they belong in the classroom.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the speaker imagine? (a world where young people manage their own money). What do critics say? (schools are overloaded).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker confident or hesitant? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the attitude, such as so they belong.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Two-minute persuasive speech: the student delivers a short speech to the tutor, who acts as a sceptical audience member and asks one challenging question at the end. The student must use the five-part frame.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Imagine ...
  • My view is that ...
  • Ultimately, ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner and sceptical listener. Challenge the student to defend the speech, and share one stronger phrase live in the chat while they speak.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Imagine a school, the kids dont know money.
  • My opinion is that money education is important very much.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
