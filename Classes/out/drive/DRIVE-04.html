1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this statement: Plastic bags should be banned in supermarkets. Ask: Do you agree? Give one reason in one sentence.

Tutor action: Give a short answer, then ask the student to restate their reason in a more formal way.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: It is + past participle + that (formal reporting); subject + is / was / has been + past participle (passive for focus on the action)

Examples

  • It is widely believed that recycling reduces waste.
  • The data has been analysed by three independent teams.
  • Several solutions were proposed during the meeting.

Execution method: Show a casual sentence beside its formal passive version. Ask guiding questions: Who does the action? Does the action matter more than the person? Let the student rewrite one casual sentence in the formal style.

Check question: In a formal report, do we usually name the person who did the action, or focus on the action?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this report extract twice at native speed: It has been estimated that the new policy could save up to two million pounds a year. However, concerns were raised that the figures had not been independently verified.

Direct fact check

Ask: What could the policy save? (up to two million pounds a year). What was not independently verified? (the figures).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the report sound confident or cautious? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the level of certainty, such as it has been estimated.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Briefing: the student presents a short, formal briefing on a topic of their choice (a policy, a product, a local issue) to the tutor, using at least three passive structures. The tutor asks two questions as a board member would.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • It is widely believed that ...
  • It has been suggested that ...
  • Concerns were raised about ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner. Challenge the student to use a formal register, and share a more precise word or phrase live in the chat as they speak.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • It is believe that recycling is good.
  • The data have analysed by three teams.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
