1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: Is it better to work in one job for many years, or to change jobs often? Give your view in thirty seconds.

Tutor action: Let the student answer, then ask one question that forces a contrast: What is the drawback of your view?

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Albeit + clause (concession, a small qualification); whereby + clause (a process or mechanism); nonetheless / notwithstanding + idea (despite that); consequently + result

Examples

  • The plan succeeded, albeit on a smaller scale than expected.
  • The fund supports projects whereby local groups share the costs.
  • The evidence was limited; nonetheless, the committee approved the change.

Execution method: Show each marker beside a plain version using but or so. Ask guiding questions: Is the second idea a concession, a mechanism, or a result? Then ask the student to rewrite one sentence from their warm-up with a new marker.

Check question: Does notwithstanding introduce a result or a concession?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at native speed: The reform passed, albeit narrowly. Nonetheless, critics argue that its rules are unclear, whereby small firms may struggle. Consequently, the government has promised a review next year.

Direct fact check

Ask: How did the reform pass? (narrowly). What do critics say about the rules? (they are unclear). What has the government promised? (a review next year).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the writer optimistic or sceptical about the reform? Ask the student to point to the marker that signals the concession, such as albeit or nonetheless.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Balanced argument: the student argues one side of the warm-up question using albeit, nonetheless, and consequently at least once each. The tutor counters with the opposite view and asks: Which marker shows your strongest point?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Albeit ...
  • Nonetheless, ...
  • Consequently, ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a debate partner and language coach. Let the student finish each argument, and share one more precise discourse marker live in the chat when they repeat but or so.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The plan succeeded but on a smaller scale.
  • The evidence was limited, so the committee approved it, but it was still the right choice.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
