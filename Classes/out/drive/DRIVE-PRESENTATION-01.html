1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this topic: Should the company move its entire operation to remote work? Ask: What would be your opening line to a board?

Tutor action: Let the student write or say one opening line, then ask them to make it more striking.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Hook (rhetorical question / striking figure / vivid scenario) + thesis (My recommendation is that ... / I will argue that ...) + roadmap (I will cover three points)

Examples

  • How much of our budget disappears into empty desks every month?
  • Imagine a team that never has to commute, yet meets its targets every quarter.
  • My recommendation is that we move to a hybrid model, and I will cover three reasons.

Execution method: Show the three hooks with the thesis and roadmap. Ask guiding questions: Which hook would catch the attention of a busy board? Where does the thesis appear? Then ask the student to write a hook, a thesis, and a roadmap for the warm-up topic.

Check question: Which part of the opening states the recommendation the speaker will defend?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this presentation opening twice at native speed: Imagine a company that spends millions on offices no one uses. Our proposal is simple: reduce the floor space and invest in flexible tools. I will argue that this is the most cost-effective choice, and I will cover three reasons.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the company spend millions on? (offices no one uses). What is the proposal? (reduce floor space and invest in flexible tools). How many reasons will the speaker cover? (three).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker cautious or persuasive? Ask the student to point to the phrase that signals the argument, such as I will argue that.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Opening challenge: the student writes and delivers a 60-second opening for a business recommendation of their choice, using a hook, a thesis, and a roadmap. The tutor plays a sceptical board member and asks one challenging question after the opening.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • How much of ... ?
  • Imagine ...
  • My recommendation is that ..., and I will cover ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a sceptical board member. Respond to the opening with a challenge, and share one stronger hook in the chat when the student opens with a flat statement.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Today I will talk about remote work and I have some ideas.
  • Imagine, the office is big and the money is a lot.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
