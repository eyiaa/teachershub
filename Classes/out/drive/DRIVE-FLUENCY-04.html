1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this statement: Companies should measure success by profit alone. Ask: Give your position in thirty seconds.

Tutor action: Start a thirty-second timer, then ask the student to name the strongest counter-argument.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Claim + reason + example + concession + response: I would argue that ... / The strongest reason is ... / Admittedly, ... / Nevertheless, ...

Examples

  • I would argue that profit is necessary but not sufficient.
  • Admittedly, a focus on profit can drive efficiency, and it does reward investors.
  • Nevertheless, a business that ignores its staff and customers rarely lasts long.

Execution method: Show the five-part frame with the example phrases. Ask guiding questions: Which part states the claim? Where does the concession appear, and how is it answered? Then ask the student to speak for 90 seconds on the warm-up statement using the frame.

Check question: In Admittedly, a focus on profit can drive efficiency, does the speaker accept the counter-argument, or reject it?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this opinion extract twice at native speed: Critics argue that measuring firms by profit alone encourages short-term thinking. Admittedly, shareholders have a legitimate claim to returns. Nevertheless, long-term profitability depends on trust, and trust depends on how a company treats its people.

Direct fact check

Ask: What do critics argue? (profit alone encourages short-term thinking). What do shareholders have? (a legitimate claim to returns). What does long-term profitability depend on? (trust).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer accept the shareholders' view fully? Ask the student to point to the phrase that concedes a point before answering it, such as Admittedly.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Timed opinion: the tutor gives a new business statement. The student speaks for 90 seconds using the five-part frame, then the tutor challenges the concession with one counter-argument. The student responds in 30 seconds, then repeats the full answer once with a faster, smoother delivery.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I would argue that ...
  • Admittedly, ...
  • Nevertheless, ...

Tutor mandate

Keep time visibly. Do not interrupt the 90 seconds. After the response round, note one fluency gain and one priority for the next attempt.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I think profit is important, but people is important too, so yes.
  • Admittedly the profit good, and also the staff is good, and in fact very good.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
