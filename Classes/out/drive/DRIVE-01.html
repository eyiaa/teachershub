1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this statement on screen: Working from home is better than working in an office. Ask: Do you agree? Defend your answer in one sentence.

Tutor action: Challenge the student gently with one counter-point, such as But what about teamwork? Let the student respond instantly.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Claim + reason + example + concession (I believe X, because Y. For example, Z. Admittedly, W.)

Examples

  • I believe remote work is effective, because it saves commuting time.
  • Admittedly, some teams lose focus at home, so good communication matters.
  • Nevertheless, the overall benefits outweigh the drawbacks.

Execution method: Show the argument frame on screen. Ask guiding questions: Which part is the claim? Where is the concession? Let the student rebuild their warm-up answer using the frame, adding a concession.

Check question: Which connector introduces the concession: admittedly, because, or for example?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Play a 45-second clip from a business podcast with a fast native speaker, or read this text twice at native speed: The shift to remote work sounds like a win for everyone, but managers are quietly worried. Productivity is harder to measure, and junior staff often miss the informal learning that happens in an office.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is the main concern of managers? (productivity is harder to measure). Which group misses informal learning? (junior staff).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the speaker sound convinced that remote work is a win, or doubtful? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the tone, such as sounds like a win.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Structured debate: the student takes the side they chose in the warm-up and defends it against the tutor, who argues the opposite. Each turn, the student must give a claim, a reason, and an example. Finish with a two-minute summary of the best argument on either side.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I would argue that ...
  • The strongest point is ...
  • That said, ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a conversation partner and language coach. Share one better word or phrase live in the chat as the student speaks, and do not stop their flow.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I think that remote work is good because it is more comfortable.
  • The bad thing is the team don't talk.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
