1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this topic: Should the company move its office to a smaller building? Ask: Give your position in thirty seconds.

Tutor action: Ask for an immediate position, then challenge it with one counter-argument.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Hook + thesis (with a cleft) + two points (one with inversion) + concession + closing call to action

Examples

  • Imagine paying rent for space nobody uses. (hook)
  • What we need is a smaller, more flexible office. (cleft thesis)
  • Not only would we save money, but we would also reduce waste. (inversion point)

Execution method: Show the five-part frame with the example lines. Ask guiding questions: Which part highlights the key idea? Where is the concession? Then ask the student to map their warm-up position onto the frame.

Check question: Which part of the pitch should highlight the main idea with a cleft?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this short pitch extract twice at native speed: Imagine a company that pays for empty desks. It is the wasted space that drains our budget, and not only is that expensive, it also slows decisions. What we need is a leaner plan.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the company pay for? (empty desks). What drains the budget? (the wasted space). What does the speaker say we need? (a leaner plan).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker confident or hesitant? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the strongest argument, such as not only is that expensive.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Five-minute pitch: the student delivers a short persuasive pitch to the tutor, who acts as a sceptical executive and asks one challenging question at the end. The pitch must use the five-part frame, a cleft, and an inverted structure.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Imagine ...
  • What we need is ...
  • Not only ..., but also ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a sceptical audience member and coach. Challenge the student to defend the pitch, and share one stronger phrase live in the chat while they speak.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Imagine a office with empty desks, it is bad.
  • What we need is that we have a smaller office.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
