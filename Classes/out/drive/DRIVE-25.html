1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this question: What is the most important point to include when you summarise a long meeting for a busy director?

Tutor action: Let the student give one or two priorities, then ask which of these could be cut.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Lead with the conclusion, compress detail with participles, and refer back with the former, the latter, or such measures

Examples

  • Sales rose 12 percent, driven by the new range.
  • Having cut costs by a fifth, the division now meets its targets.
  • Two risks remain. The former is financial; the latter is reputational.

Execution method: Show a long sentence beside a compressed version. Ask guiding questions: What is the main message? What detail can be removed without losing meaning? Then ask the student to compress one sentence from their warm-up answer.

Check question: Which part of the sentence Sales rose 12 percent, driven by the new range can be cut without changing the main message?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this report extract twice at native speed: Over the past year, the division expanded into three new markets. Costs rose sharply, and margins fell in two of them. Management has since reviewed its pricing, and results have begun to improve.

Direct fact check

Ask: How many new markets did the division expand into? (three). In how many markets did margins fall? (two). What has management reviewed? (its pricing).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the report optimistic or cautious? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the direction of results, such as have begun to improve.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Summary pitch: the student summarises the report extract to the tutor, who plays a busy director, in under forty seconds. The director asks one question about what was left out.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Overall, ...
  • Having ..., the division ...
  • The main risk is ...

Tutor mandate

Act as a busy director and coach. Interrupt only at the end of each summary, and share one more concise phrase live in the chat while the student speaks.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The division it expanded to three markets and the costs was going up very fast.
  • This report is about the division and it says the results are getting better.

Tutor checklist

☐ Did the student defend a stance without translating from their native language?

☐ Did I share better vocabulary live while the student spoke?

☐ Is the student talking for 75-85% of the class?

☐ Did the wrap-up review good vs. better word choices?
