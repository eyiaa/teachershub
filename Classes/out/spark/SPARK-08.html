Shopping and Prices: How Much Is It?

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What's your favorite food? What do you usually drink? Then ask: Where do you usually buy food? Name two things you buy at a store.

Tutor action: Link food to shopping. Accept short answers and expand them into full sentences.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: How much is this / that + one item? It's ... / How much are these / those + two or more items? They're ... + Do you have ...? Can I have ...? I'd like ..., please.

Examples

  • How much is this book? It's twelve dollars.
  • How much are these shoes? They're twenty-five dollars.
  • That bag is black. Those books are expensive.
  • Do you have a blue T-shirt? Yes, we do.

Execution method: Show stores (supermarket, clothing store, bookstore, pharmacy) and products (bread, milk, T-shirt, shoes, book, bag). Read prices together: $5.00 is five dollars, and $18.50 is eighteen dollars and fifty cents. Use gestures to show near and far, and one and many. Gap-fill: ___ book is $10 (one, near). ___ shoes are $30 (many, near). ___ bag is $20 (one, far). ___ books are $15 (many, far).

Check question: One item near you: this or these? Many items far away: that or those?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this shop dialogue twice, slowly: Salesperson: Hello! Can I help you? Customer: Yes, please. Do you have a blue T-shirt? Salesperson: Yes, we do. Customer: How much is it? Salesperson: It's fifteen dollars. Customer: Great! And how much are those shoes? Salesperson: They're forty-five dollars. Customer: I'd like the T-shirt, please.

Direct fact check

Ask: What color is the T-shirt? (blue). How much is it? (fifteen dollars). How much are the shoes? (forty-five dollars). What does the customer buy? (the T-shirt).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Do you think the shoes are cheap or expensive for the customer? Why? Accept a simple reason.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: At a clothing store: the tutor is the salesperson. The student greets, asks if a product is available, asks the price of two items, points to one with this, that, these, or those, chooses one, buys it politely, and says thank you. Then switch roles. Use these prices: blue T-shirt $15, black T-shirt $20, small bag $25, large bag $40.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Do you have a ...?
  • How much is this ...? / How much are these ...?
  • It's ... dollars. / They're ... dollars.
  • Can I have this one, please? / I'd like ..., please.
  • Thank you! You're welcome.

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Say prices clearly and check that the student understands them. Let the student make their own choices. Note errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • How much is these shoes?
  • This shoes are twenty dollars.
  • Is fifteen dollars.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
