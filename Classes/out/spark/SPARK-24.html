Advice and Obligations: Should, Have To, and Don't Have To

ClassSPARK-24

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: How are you feeling today? Then show a picture of a tired student and ask: What should she do? Does she have to go to school today?

Tutor action: Use the picture to model should. Accept any advice, then say it back as a full sentence with should.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Advice: should / shouldn't + base verb (You should rest. You shouldn't drive.). Obligation: have to / has to + base verb (I have to wear a uniform. She has to finish the report.). No need: don't have to / doesn't have to (You don't have to drive.). Questions: Do I have to ...? Should I ...?

Examples

  • You should rest. You have to wear a helmet.
  • You shouldn't drive when you feel tired.
  • I have to wear a uniform at work.
  • You don't have to drive. You can take the bus.

Execution method: Show three pictures: a helmet on a bike, a No Parking sign, and a person with a cold. Ask what the person should do, and model the answer. For rules, show two signs and ask: Do we have to follow this rule? Contrast have to, which is a rule or necessity, with should, which is a good idea. Explain that don't have to means it is not necessary, not that it is forbidden. The student completes: You ___ rest (should). I ___ wear a helmet (have to). You ___ drive (don't have to).

Check question: Does don't have to mean you are not allowed, or that you do not need to?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this conversation twice, slowly: Doctor: You look tired, Mark. You should sleep more and drink more water. Mark: Thanks. I have to work late every day. Doctor: You shouldn't work late every night. Try to finish earlier. Mark: OK. Do I have to take medicine? Doctor: No, you don't have to. But you should eat more vegetables.

Direct fact check

Ask: What should Mark do more? (sleep more and drink more water). What does Mark have to do every day? (work late). Does Mark have to take medicine? (No). What should he eat more of? (vegetables).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the doctor worried about Mark? Which words show it? (You look tired, You should sleep more).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Give advice and explain a rule: the tutor describes a problem, such as a sore back from sitting all day, or a new job with strict rules. The student gives three recommendations with should or shouldn't, and explains two obligations with have to or don't have to. The tutor then asks one follow-up question, such as Why?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • You should ... / You shouldn't ...
  • I have to ... / He has to ...
  • You don't have to ...
  • Why do I have to ...?
  • It's a good idea to ...

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use problems the student knows, such as study or work. Keep the advice short. Record three recommendations and two obligations on a 1 to 4 scale for accuracy and task completion, and note whether the student separates advice from obligation.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • You should resting more.
  • I has to wear a uniform.
  • You don't have drive.
