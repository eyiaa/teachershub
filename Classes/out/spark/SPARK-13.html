My Future Plans: What Are You Going to Do?

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What did you do last weekend? Then ask: What are you going to do next weekend? Give an example: Next Saturday, I'm going to visit my grandmother.

Tutor action: Accept any answer and say it back with going to. Show the move from yesterday to tomorrow on a quick timeline.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: I am (I'm) / You are / He is / She is + going to + base verb + tomorrow / next weekend / on Saturday. Negative: I'm not going to ... Questions: What are you going to do? Are you going to ...? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.

Examples

  • I'm going to visit my family on Sunday.
  • She's going to study English tomorrow.
  • I'm not going to work next weekend.
  • Are you going to watch the game? Yes, I am.

Execution method: Put three sentences on a timeline: Yesterday, I watched a movie. Today, I study English. Tomorrow, I'm going to play soccer. Ask the student which one is about the future. Show going to + verb with I, you, he, and she. The student makes three sentences about their own plans, one of them negative. Then practise both question forms.

Check question: Do we say I going to visit or I'm going to visit?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this phone call twice, slowly: Sam: Hi, Lisa! What are you going to do this weekend? Lisa: On Saturday morning, I'm going to clean my apartment. In the afternoon, I'm free. Sam: Great! Are you going to come to the park? Lisa: Yes! What time? Sam: At three o'clock. Lisa: Perfect. See you on Saturday!

Direct fact check

Ask: What is Lisa going to do on Saturday morning? (clean her apartment). Is she free in the afternoon? (Yes.) Where are they going to meet? (at the park). What time? (three o'clock).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is Lisa happy about the plan? Which words show it? (Yes!, Perfect).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Weekend planning: the student has a weekend calendar with two activities and some free time. The tutor plays a friend who wants to meet. The student describes their plans with going to, asks when the tutor is free, suggests an activity, and agrees on a day and time. Then the tutor suggests a time that does not work, and the student says no politely and offers another time.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I'm going to ... on ...
  • I'm not going to ...
  • What are you going to do on Saturday?
  • Are you free on ...? / Are you going to ...?
  • Let's meet at ... See you then!

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use a real or an imaginary calendar. Let the student lead the planning, and note errors with going to for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I going to visit my family.
  • She is going to studies tomorrow.
  • Are you going to come? Yes, I going.
