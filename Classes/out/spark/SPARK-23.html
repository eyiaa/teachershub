Food, Shopping, and Quantities: Some, Any, Much, and Many

ClassSPARK-23

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: Where do you buy food? Then show a fridge with milk, eggs, apples, and rice, and ask: What do you have at home? Is there any milk?

Tutor action: Use the picture as the context. Accept lists of food words, and repeat them in a full sentence such as We have some eggs.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Countable nouns (eggs, apples, tomatoes): many, a few, some, any. Uncountable nouns (rice, milk, bread): much, a little, some, any. Some in positive sentences; any in questions and negatives. How many eggs? How much milk? A few and a little mean a small amount.

Examples

  • We need some rice and a few tomatoes.
  • How much milk do we have?
  • Is there any bread?
  • There aren't many eggs, but there's a little cheese.

Execution method: Sort food pictures into two columns: countable (eggs, apples, tomatoes, bananas) and uncountable (rice, milk, bread, water, cheese). Point out that uncountable nouns have no plural. Use the fridge picture: ask How many eggs are there? (a few) and How much milk is there? (a little). Show some for positive sentences and any for questions and negatives. The student completes: We need ___ rice. Do we have ___ tomatoes? There aren't ___ apples.

Check question: Do we say How much eggs or How many eggs?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this shopping conversation twice, slowly: Mum: Can you buy some bread, please? Son: Sure! Is there any milk in the fridge? Mum: No, there isn't any. Please buy a little milk and a few eggs. Also, get some apples. We don't have many apples, and we need some fruit for the week. Son: How much money do we have? Mum: Not much, so be careful!

Direct fact check

Ask: What does Mum ask the son to buy first? (some bread). Is there any milk in the fridge? (No). How many eggs does she want? (a few). Do they have many apples? (No). How much money do they have? (not much).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is Mum worried about money? Which words show it? (Not much, so be careful).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Grocery order: the student has a shopping list of four items and a budget. The tutor is the shop assistant. The student orders the four items with the right quantities, using some, any, a few, a little, How many, or How much. Then the tutor asks if the student needs anything else, and the student asks Do you have any ...?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I'd like some ... / Do you have any ...?
  • How much ... / How many ...?
  • A few ... / A little ...
  • Is there any ...? No, there isn't any.
  • That's all, thank you.

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use a real shopping list or a picture menu. Let the student choose the quantities, and correct only errors that stop the shop assistant from understanding. Mark the grocery order on a 1 to 4 scale, including whether the quantities fit the list.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • How many milk do we need?
  • We need much eggs.
  • Is there some bread?
