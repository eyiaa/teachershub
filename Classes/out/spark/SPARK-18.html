Talking About the Past: More Irregular Verbs

ClassSPARK-18
Student name

Target goalUse -ed endings for regular verbs and irregular forms such as took, bought, met, made, and felt, and link events with first, then, and after that.

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What did you do last weekend? Then say: Last weekend, I visited my cousins. Ask: Where did you go? Who did you see?

Tutor action: Accept any weekend, real or invented. Say the answer back in the past simple, then ask the student to repeat the verb form after you.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Regular verbs: base + -ed (worked, visited, watched, studied). Irregular verbs have their own past form (took, bought, met, made, felt). Time words: yesterday, last night, last weekend, last month.

Examples

  • Last Saturday I visited my cousins.
  • We cooked dinner and watched a movie.
  • I took the train to the city and bought a jacket.
  • I met my friend, and we felt very happy.

Execution method: Show verb pairs on screen: visit / visited, cook / cooked, watch / watched, study / studied (y changes to i), stop / stopped (double consonant). Then show irregular pairs: take / took, buy / bought, meet / met, make / made, feel / felt. The student reads each base verb and says the past form aloud, then builds one sentence with it. Point out that irregular forms must be learned in sets, so repeat each set of three.

Check question: What is the past form of buy: buyed or bought?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice, slowly: Last weekend, Tom visited his grandmother. He took the bus to her house on Saturday morning. They cooked lunch together and talked for hours. In the afternoon, Tom bought some flowers for her. In the evening, he met his cousins at a café. He felt tired but happy.

Direct fact check

Ask: Who did Tom visit? (his grandmother). How did he travel? (by bus). What did he buy? (some flowers). Who did he meet in the evening? (his cousins). How did he feel? (tired but happy).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Did Tom have a good weekend? Which words show it? (happy, talked for hours).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: My best weekend: the student tells the tutor about a recent weekend, real or invented, with at least five past actions in order. The tutor asks follow-up questions such as Who did you see? and How did you feel? The student links at least three events with first, then, and after that.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Last weekend, I ...
  • I took / bought / met / made ...
  • First, I ... Then, I ... After that, I ...
  • How did you feel?
  • I felt happy / tired / bored.

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Ask for the story, not perfect verb forms. Let the student finish each part before you respond. Count the past actions the student recounts, target five, and mark task completion, interaction, intelligibility, and accuracy on a 1 to 4 scale.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Last weekend I go to the market.
  • I buyed some bread.
  • Then I meet my friend and we are happy.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
