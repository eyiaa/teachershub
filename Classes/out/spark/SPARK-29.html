Making Requests and Invitations: Can, Could, and Would

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What do you usually do on weekends? Then ask: Would you like to get a coffee this weekend? Show a picture of two friends in a café.

Tutor action: Model the invitation first, then wait for the student to answer. Accept a yes or a no, and ask Why not? to lead into the next step.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Requests: Can you help me? / Could you help me, please? (more polite). Invitations: Would you like to + base verb? / Would you like a + noun? Responses: Yes, I'd love to! / I'm sorry, I can't. I have to work. / That sounds great, but ...

Examples

  • Could you help me, please? Of course!
  • Would you like to see a movie on Friday?
  • I'd love to, but I can't. I have to work.
  • Can I have a glass of water, please?

Execution method: Show three request cards: help with a bag, a menu, and a phone number. Model Could you ...? and Can I ...? Show that Would you like ...? is an invitation. Give a yes response (I'd love to!) and a polite no (I'm sorry, I can't. I have to study.). The student practises with a role card: one invitation, one request for help, and one refusal.

Check question: Do we say Would you like go or Would you like to go?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this conversation twice, slowly: Ana: Hi, Tom. Would you like to come to my birthday party on Saturday? Tom: That sounds great! Thank you! Can I bring anything? Ana: Could you bring some music, please? Tom: Of course! Oh, but I'm sorry, I can't come until seven. I have to finish work first. Ana: No problem. Could you still come at seven?

Direct fact check

Ask: What is Ana inviting Tom to? (her birthday party). What does she ask him to bring? (some music). Why can't Tom come before seven? (he has to finish work). What time can he come? (seven).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Are Ana and Tom polite to each other? Which words show it? (Thank you, please, No problem).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Invite, accept, decline, and request: the student completes three role-plays. First, the student invites the tutor to lunch with Would you like to ...? Second, the student asks the tutor for help with Could you ...? Third, the student declines an invitation from the tutor, gives a reason, and suggests a different day.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Would you like to ...?
  • I'd love to! / That sounds great!
  • I'm sorry, I can't. I have to ...
  • Could you help me, please?
  • What about Thursday instead?

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Play a busy friend who accepts some invitations and declines others. Note whether the student completes all three role-plays and gives a reason when declining. Mark each role-play on a 1 to 4 scale.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Would you like come to lunch?
  • Can you helping me, please?
  • I'm sorry, I no can.
