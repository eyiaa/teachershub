Presentation Skills 1: Introducing a Simple Topic

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What is one topic about your work or studies that you could explain to a group?

Tutor action: Let the student name a topic, then give a short example: My topic is my office.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Hello, everyone. This is my ... / I'd like to show you ... / Let's begin.

Examples

  • Hello, everyone. This is my job.
  • I'd like to show you my office.
  • Let's begin with my team.

Execution method: Show the opening phrases on screen. Ask guiding questions: Which phrase introduces the topic? Which phrase says you will show something? Then ask the student to write a two-line opening for their topic.

Check question: Which phrase tells the listener you will show them something?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this opening twice at a steady pace: Hello, everyone. This is my company. I'd like to show you our office and our team. Let's begin with the office.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is the presentation about? (the speaker's company). What will the speaker show? (the office and the team). Where do they begin? (the office).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the speaker sound friendly or formal? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows a friendly start, such as Hello, everyone.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Short opening: the student gives a 30-second opening to you, playing a friendly audience member. The student introduces the topic, says what they will show, and ends with Let's begin.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • This is my ...
  • I'd like to show you ...
  • Let's begin.

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Act as a friendly audience, and praise each complete sentence before correcting.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • This is my job, I show you the office.
  • I like show you the office, lets begin.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
