My Home and Neighborhood: There Is, There Are, and Where Things Are

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: Where do you buy food? Then ask: Do you live in a house or an apartment? What's your favorite room?

Tutor action: Do not ask for personal details the student may not want to share. Accept short answers and expand them into full sentences.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: There is + a / an + one thing. / There are + two or more things. + in / on / under / next to / between + place. Where is ...? It's ... / Where are ...? They're ... / Is there ...? Are there ...?

Examples

  • There is a sofa in my living room.
  • There are two chairs in the kitchen.
  • The lamp is on the table. The shoes are under the bed.
  • The chair is between the table and the sofa.

Execution method: Show a house picture with a bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, living room, and dining room, and link each room to a routine verb: I cook in the kitchen. Add furniture: sofa, table, chair, TV, bed, lamp, closet, mirror. Show one object for there is and several for there are. Then move a pen around a book to show in, on, under, next to, and between, and ask: Where is the pen?

Check question: Two chairs: there is or there are?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this description twice, slowly, while the student looks at a simple room picture: This is my living room. There is a big sofa next to the window. There are two lamps. The TV is on a small table. My cat is under the sofa! Near my home, there is a park and a supermarket.

Direct fact check

Ask: Where is the sofa? (next to the window). How many lamps are there? (two). Where is the TV? (on a small table). Where is the cat? (under the sofa). What is near the home? (a park and a supermarket).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker happy with the home? What is funny in the text? (the cat under the sofa).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Help me find it: the tutor is visiting the student's home and can't find a phone, a bag, and a book. The tutor asks Where is my phone?, and the student explains with prepositions, without pointing. Then switch roles, using a room picture. Finish: the student describes two rooms and one place near the home.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • There is a ... / There are ... in my ...
  • It's in / on / under / next to / between ...
  • Where is ...? / Where are ...?
  • Is there a ...? Yes, there is. / No, there isn't.
  • There is a ... near my home.

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Offer a picture or an imaginary home if the student prefers. Give feedback after the activity unless the student is stuck.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • There is two chairs in the kitchen.
  • The lamp is in the table.
  • In my house have a big kitchen.
