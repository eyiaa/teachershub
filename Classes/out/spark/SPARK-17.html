Everyday Routines and Changes: Present Simple and Present Continuous

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What did you do recently? Tell me two things about your week. Then ask: What do you usually do on Mondays? What are you doing differently this week?

Tutor action: Keep the warm-up relaxed. Accept short answers, expand one into a present simple sentence and one into a present continuous sentence, then move on.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Habit: subject + present simple + adverb of frequency + main verb (I usually work from home). Now: subject + am / is / are + verb-ing (I'm working at the office this week). Negative: I don't work / I'm not working. Questions: Do you work ...? / Are you working ...?

Examples

  • I usually work from home, but this week I'm working at the office.
  • She always takes the bus, but today she's walking.
  • We don't study on Sundays, but this weekend we're studying for a test.
  • Do you usually cook dinner? Are you cooking tonight?

Execution method: Draw two columns on screen: Routine (every day, usually) and Right now (today, this week). Read one model sentence and ask which part is a habit and which part is a temporary change. Then gap-fill orally: I ___ (work) from home on Mondays. This week I ___ (work) at the office. Show where always, usually, sometimes, and never go: before the main verb, but after am, is, or are. Use a frequency scale from always to never to practise placement.

Check question: Which sentence is about a habit: I'm studying English or I study English every day?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice, slowly: Hi, I'm Lucia. I usually work from home on Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday. On Wednesdays, I go to the office. This week I'm working at the office every day because my manager is away. I always have coffee in the morning, but this week I'm drinking tea. I don't usually eat lunch at my desk, but today I'm eating a sandwich at my desk!

Direct fact check

Ask: Where does Lucia usually work? (from home on Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday). Where is she working this week? (at the office every day). What does she usually drink in the morning? (coffee). What is she drinking this week? (tea).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is this a normal week for Lucia? How do you know? Accept a short answer such as no, because of this week and because her manager is away.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: What is different this week? The student describes three usual activities with adverbs of frequency, then three temporary changes with the present continuous. The tutor plays a friend and asks a follow-up question for each change, such as Why are you doing that this week?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I usually ... / I always ... / I sometimes ...
  • This week, I'm ...
  • I don't usually ..., but today I'm ...
  • What are you doing differently this week?
  • Why are you doing that this week?

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Respond naturally and prompt the student to say more rather than giving answers. Note one strength and one recurring gap. Count the routine and current contrasts the student makes, target four, and mark task completion, interaction, intelligibility, and accuracy on a 1 to 4 scale.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I am usually work from home.
  • This week I work at the office.
  • He is go to the office today.

Tutor checklist
