1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What is one thing you like about your job or your studies?

Tutor action: Set a 30-second timer and start it when the student begins.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: I think + opinion. For example, + example. (30 seconds: one opinion and one example)

Examples

  • I think teamwork is important. For example, we solve problems faster together.
  • I like my study group. For example, we help each other with homework.
  • I think English is useful. For example, I can talk to customers.

Execution method: Show the frame and the 30-second timer. Ask guiding questions: What is the opinion? What is the example? Then ask the student to answer a short question in 30 seconds with the frame.

Check question: Which part of the answer gives an example?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this answer twice at a steady pace: I think my job is good. For example, I work with nice people, and we help each other. I also like the hours, because I finish at four.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the speaker think about the job? (it is good). What is one example? (they work with nice people). What do they like about the hours? (they finish at four)

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the speaker sound positive? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the opinion, such as I think my job is good.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Thirty-second answer: the tutor asks one question about work or study, such as: Do you like your job? The student answers in 30 seconds using the frame. The student then tries again with a new example, faster.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I think ...
  • For example, ...
  • I like ... because ...

Tutor mandate

Keep time visibly. Do not interrupt during the 30 seconds. After each answer, praise one clear point and give one tip for the next try.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I think the job is good, the job is good, the job.
  • For example the people is nice, and we help.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
