Predictions and Decisions: Using Will

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What are you going to do next weekend? Then show a picture of dark clouds and ask: What do you think will happen this afternoon?

Tutor action: Accept a guess, even an unlikely one. Say it back with I think it will ..., and ask the student what they think.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Subject + will + base verb (It will rain. I will help you.). Contractions: I'll, you'll, it'll, won't. Use will for predictions based on opinion, and for decisions made while speaking. Negative: will not / won't. Questions: Will you ...? Yes, I will. / No, I won't. Add I think, I hope, or probably before will.

Examples

  • I think it will rain tomorrow. I'll bring an umbrella.
  • Oh, the phone is ringing. I'll answer it.
  • Don't worry. I won't be late.
  • Will you come to the party? Yes, I will.

Execution method: Show a weather picture for a prediction: ask What will happen? and model I think it will rain. Then show a ringing phone for a decision: I'll answer it. Contrast with going to, which uses evidence: Look at the clouds. It's going to rain. Practise the sound of 'll in I'll, you'll, and we'll. Practise short answers: Will you ...? Yes, I will.

Check question: The phone is ringing. Do we use will or going to for a decision made at this moment?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this conversation twice, slowly: Ben: Look at those dark clouds! Anna: I think it will rain soon. Ben: You're right. I'll take my umbrella. Anna: Do you want a lift? Ben: Oh, yes, please. That would be great. I'll wait for you at the bus stop. Anna: Don't worry, I won't be late!

Direct fact check

Ask: What does Anna think will happen? (it will rain soon). What will Ben take? (his umbrella). Where will Ben wait? (at the bus stop). Will Anna be late? (No, she won't).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Are Ben and Anna friendly? Which words show it? (Oh, yes, please, Don't worry, That would be great).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Predictions and decisions: the tutor describes everyday situations. The student makes four predictions about the future, such as the weather, the news, or next year, and responds to two immediate decisions. For example, the tutor says: I'm really hungry. The student offers help with I'll make some lunch.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I think it will ...
  • I don't think it will ...
  • Probably, ... will ...
  • I'll ... / I won't ...
  • Will you ...? Yes, I will. / No, I won't.

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Accept opinions even if they are unlikely. Count the predictions the student makes, target four, and check whether the student uses will for decisions made while speaking. Mark interaction and accuracy on a 1 to 4 scale.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I think it will to rain tomorrow.
  • I think it will rains tomorrow.
  • I won't going to be late.

Tutor checklist
