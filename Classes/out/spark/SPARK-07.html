Food and Drinks: What Do You Like to Eat?

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What do you like doing in your free time? Do you like cooking? Then ask: What's your favorite food?

Tutor action: Let the student name one food. Say it back as a full sentence, such as You like pizza.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: For breakfast / lunch / dinner, I have ... + a / an + things you can count (an apple, two eggs) or some + things you can't count (some rice, some water) + Would you like ...? I'd like ..., please.

Examples

  • I have eggs and bread for breakfast.
  • I'd like an apple and some water, please.
  • I prefer tea, but I like coffee too.

Execution method: Show food and drink pictures: bread, rice, chicken, eggs, fruit, vegetables, water, milk, coffee, tea, juice. The student says what they eat for each meal. Show two columns: things you can count (an apple, two bananas) and things you can't count (some rice, some milk). Practise: an apple or some apple? two eggs or two egg? a water or some water? Then model Would you like ...? and I'd like ...

Check question: Do we say a water or some water?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this café dialogue twice, slowly: Server: Hello! Would you like something to drink? Customer: Yes, please. I'd like some orange juice. Server: Would you like something to eat? Customer: Yes, please. I'd like a chicken sandwich. Server: Here you are. Customer: Thank you!

Direct fact check

Ask: What drink does the customer order? (orange juice). What food does the customer order? (a chicken sandwich).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the customer polite? Which words show it? (please, thank you).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: At a café: the tutor is the server. The student greets the server, orders one food and one drink with I'd like ..., and uses please and thank you. Then switch roles. Finish with the student's food profile: three foods they usually eat, two drinks they enjoy, one food they don't like, and one sentence with prefer.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • For breakfast, I have ...
  • I like ... / I don't like ... / I prefer ...
  • Would you like ...? Yes, please. / No, thank you.
  • I'd like a ... / some ..., please.

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Respect food and cultural choices. Model the polite forms once, then let the student order alone. Note errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I want a water.
  • I'd like two egg, please.
  • For breakfast, I eat a rice.

Tutor checklist

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?
