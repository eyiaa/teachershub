1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: Do you like your job or your studies? Why?

Tutor action: Give a short example: I like my job because I meet people. Let the student answer, even with one sentence.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Sentence 1 + and (add) / but (contrast) / so (result) / because (reason) + sentence 2

Examples

  • I like my job, and I work with a good team.
  • The work is hard, but I enjoy it.
  • I was tired, so I went home early.

Execution method: Show each connector with its meaning. Ask guiding questions: Does this word add, contrast, or give a result? Then ask the student to join two short sentences about their work using because.

Check question: Does so show a reason or a result?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: I work in an office, and I like my team. The work is busy, but it is interesting. I finish late, so I often walk home. I like walking because it helps me relax.

Direct fact check

Ask: Where does the speaker work? (in an office). Why does the speaker walk home? (they finish late, so they walk). Why do they walk? (it helps them relax).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker happy with the job? Ask the student to point to the word that shows a difference, such as but, and to the reason with because.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Opinion and reason: the student tells the tutor about their job or studies in three sentences, using and, but, so, and because. The tutor asks one question: Why do you like it?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I like ... because ...
  • It is hard, but ...
  • I ..., so ...

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Let the student finish each sentence, and praise each connector they use correctly.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I like my job, because and I meet people.
  • I was tired, so I went home because it was late.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
