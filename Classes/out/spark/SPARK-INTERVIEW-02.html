1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a photo of an office with people at desks. Ask: What do people do in this office?

Tutor action: Give a short example: They answer emails. Let the student answer, even with one or two words.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: I / We / They + base verb (I work, we help); He / She + verb with s (She works); I can + base verb (I can speak English)

Examples

  • I work with customers every day.
  • She helps the team with reports.
  • I can use computers and I can speak two languages.

Execution method: Show the two patterns on screen with the s ending highlighted. Ask guiding questions: Which form do we use with she? Does can change with he or she? Then ask the student to say one job task and one skill they have.

Check question: Do we say she work or she works, and does can change?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice at a steady pace: Sam works in a shop. He helps customers and he can speak three languages. He also checks the stock every morning.

Direct fact check

Ask: Where does Sam work? (in a shop). What can he do? (speak three languages). What does he check? (the stock).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does Sam seem proud of his skills? Accept a simple answer and ask the student to point to the phrase that shows his skill, such as he can speak three languages.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Job card: the tutor shows a card with a job (a receptionist, a cook, a driver). The student says what the person does and what they can do, then says one task and one skill of their own.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I work as ...
  • I can ...
  • I help ...

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use the job cards on screen, and praise each complete sentence before correcting.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • She work in a hospital.
  • I can to speak English.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
