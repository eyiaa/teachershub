1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: Think about your first English lesson. What English can you use now? Which topics do you enjoy talking about? Tell me one thing you're proud of learning.

Tutor action: Keep the mood positive. Explain that today combines the earlier lessons, and that it is practice and evidence of progress, not a high-pressure exam.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Review of the course structures: to be (I'm from ...), present simple (I usually ...), there is / there are, I'd like ..., past simple (Yesterday, I ...), going to (Tomorrow, I'm going to ...), and Could you repeat that, please?

Examples

  • Yesterday, I watched a movie.
  • Today, I work and study English.
  • Tomorrow, I'm going to visit my family.
  • I'd like a sandwich, please. How much is it?

Execution method: Show past, present, and future on one timeline. The student says two things they did last weekend, one thing they usually do, and two things they're going to do next weekend. Then the student asks you one question about the past and one about the future. Prompt only when needed. The goal is to use familiar forms, not to learn new ones.

Check question: Which sentence is about the future: I visited my family, I visit my family, or I'm going to visit my family?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this message twice at an accessible pace, with a short pause between readings: Hi! Last Saturday, I went to the park with my sister. We walked and had lunch. Next Saturday, I'm going to meet my friend at a café at three o'clock. We're going to have coffee.

Direct fact check

Ask: What did the speaker do last Saturday? (went to the park, walked, and had lunch). Who did the speaker go with? (the sister). What is the speaker going to do next Saturday? (meet a friend at a café and have coffee). What time? (three o'clock).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker happy about next Saturday? Accept a short answer. If the student asks Could you repeat that, please?, praise it as a good strategy.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Four mini-challenges with new details, about two minutes each. 1. Meet someone new at an international event: greet, give two personal details, and ask one question. 2. Describe your life: one daily habit, one preference, and one detail about your home or family. 3. Solve a situation: buy a T-shirt for 15 dollars, order a 6-dollar sandwich and a drink, or ask the way to the library. 4. Talk about time: one past activity and one future plan. Then the student completes: I'm good at ... I want to improve ... My next English goal is ...

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Hi! My name is ... I'm from ... I work as a ...
  • I usually ... / I like ...
  • I'd like ... / How much is it? / Excuse me, how do I get to ...?
  • Last weekend, I ... Next weekend, I'm going to ...
  • Could you repeat that, please?

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Change the details from earlier lessons so the student uses the language in new situations. Do not give scripts or correct during the challenges. Record what the student did alone, then give one strength and one clear next step. Do not tell the student they have reached full A1.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Yesterday I go to the park, and tomorrow I visit my family.
  • I want a sandwich. How much it is?
  • I'm good in speaking.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
