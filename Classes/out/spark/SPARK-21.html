Plans and Arrangements: Going To and the Present Continuous

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What did you do last weekend? Then ask: What are you doing this weekend? Show a calendar with one appointment on Monday at nine.

Tutor action: Accept a short answer, then say it back as a full sentence with going to or the present continuous. Do not explain the difference yet.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Going to: am / is / are + going to + base verb, for intentions and predictions based on present evidence (I'm going to visit my family). Present continuous for arrangements: am / is / are + verb-ing + time phrase (I'm meeting my friend at six). Negative: I'm not going to visit / I'm not meeting.

Examples

  • I'm going to visit my family this weekend.
  • I'm meeting my friend at six o'clock.
  • Look at those clouds. It's going to rain.
  • We aren't going to play tennis on Sunday.

Execution method: Show a calendar with three fixed appointments: a doctor visit, a meeting, and dinner with a friend. Use the present continuous for these: I'm seeing the doctor on Monday. Then show an intention with no appointment: I'm going to study more next month. Explain that the calendar fixes the time, while going to is a plan without a fixed time. Show a sky with clouds for a prediction: It's going to rain. Ask: What are you doing on Friday? What are you going to do next year?

Check question: Is I'm meeting my friend at six a fixed arrangement or a general plan?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this message twice, slowly: Hi Sam, this is Lucy. I'm going to visit my parents this weekend, so I'm not free on Saturday. I'm meeting Ana for lunch on Sunday at one o'clock. Are you going to come? We're going to have a picnic in the park. It's going to be sunny!

Direct fact check

Ask: Where is Lucy going this weekend? (to visit her parents). When is she meeting Ana? (Sunday at one o'clock). What are they going to do? (have a picnic). What will the weather be? (sunny).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does Lucy want Sam to join them? Which words show it? (Are you going to come?, It's going to be sunny!).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Agree on a weekend plan: the student and the tutor are two friends. The student has one fixed appointment and one intention. The tutor asks about the student's plans. Together they agree on a meeting time and place, then confirm it. The student uses going to for the intention and the present continuous for the appointment.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I'm going to ... this weekend.
  • I'm meeting ... at ...
  • Are you free on ...?
  • Shall we meet at ...?
  • Does ... work for you? See you then!

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use a real calendar if the student has one. Let the student negotiate the time, and offer a time only when they are stuck. Count the plans the student describes, target three, and mark whether the arrangement is confirmed.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I going to visit my friend.
  • I'm meet my friend at six.
  • It's going to rains tomorrow.
