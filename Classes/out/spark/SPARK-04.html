My Family and Friends: My, His, Her, and Describing People

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What's your name? Where are you from? How old are you? What do you do? Then the student asks you two questions.

Tutor action: Keep it short. Watch what the student remembers, and do not repeat earlier grammar explanations.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: This is my + family word. + His / Her name is ... + He / She is + adjective (kind, funny, friendly, tall, young, happy).

Examples

  • This is my mother. Her name is Maria.
  • This is my father. His name is Carlos.
  • My sister is friendly. My brother is funny.

Execution method: Show a simple family tree with mother, father, sister, brother, grandmother, grandfather, daughter, and son. Model one introduction. Practise my, your, his, and her: This is Anna. ___ mother is a teacher. This is David. ___ brother is a student. Ask the student who the person is before they choose the word. Then add one adjective. The student may use an imaginary family.

Check question: This is David. Is it his brother or her brother?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice, slowly: This is my friend Daniel. He is 25 years old. He is from Guatemala. He is a student. He is funny and friendly. His sister is Ana. She is a nurse.

Direct fact check

Ask: How old is Daniel? (25). Where is he from? (Guatemala). What is his sister's name? (Ana). What does she do? (She is a nurse.)

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the speaker like Daniel? Which words show it? (funny, friendly).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Talking about family and friends: the student introduces two family members or friends. For each person, they give the relationship, the name, one fact, and one adjective. Then the student asks the tutor three questions, such as: Do you have a brother or sister? What's your mother's name? How old is your brother? Is your best friend funny?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • This is my ...
  • His name is ... / Her name is ...
  • He is ... / She is ... (kind, funny, tall)
  • Do you have a brother or sister? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Respect privacy and offer an imaginary family. Treat Do you have ...? as a fixed question, without explaining do. Note repeated errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • This is my sister. His name is Sofia.
  • My brother is a funny.
  • My mother she is kind.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
