1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review the last class: What's your name? Where are you from? Where do you live? Then the student asks you the same questions.

Tutor action: Keep it conversational. Check that the student remembers the language without a script, and do not reteach the last lesson unless needed.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: How old are you? I'm 25 (years old). / What do you do? I'm a / an + job. / Are you a student? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.

Examples

  • I'm thirty years old.
  • I'm a teacher. She is an engineer.
  • Are you a doctor? No, I'm not. I'm a manager.

Execution method: Model each question with your own answer, then give the short, natural answer. Show jobs with pictures: student, teacher, engineer, doctor, manager. Use a and an naturally, and give the student the word for their own job if it is missing. The student may use a made-up age. Practise the yes/no questions, then switch roles.

Check question: Do we say I'm engineer or I'm an engineer?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read these two profiles twice, slowly: This is Anna. She is 28 years old. She is from Canada. She is a doctor. This is David. He is 35. He is from Mexico. He is an engineer.

Direct fact check

Ask: How old is Anna? (28). Where is she from? (Canada). What does David do? (He is an engineer.) How old is David? (35).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is this a story, or information about two people? Accept simple answers such as information.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Meeting a new classmate: the student and tutor are new classmates in an online English course. The student greets, introduces themselves, and asks five questions about name, country, where you live, age, and job. Round 1: the tutor asks. Round 2: the student asks. Round 3: the student asks without reading. Then the student introduces Anna or David in full sentences.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • How old are you? I'm ...
  • What do you do? I'm a ... / I'm an ...
  • Where do you live? I live in ...
  • Are you a ...? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
  • This is ... She is ... / He is ...

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Reduce support each round. Let the student think before you help, and give feedback after the role-play, not during it.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I have 28 years.
  • She is doctor.
  • Are you a student? Yes, I'm.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?
