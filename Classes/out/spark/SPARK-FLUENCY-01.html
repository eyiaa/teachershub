1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: What do you do at work when you don't know an English word?

Tutor action: Give a short example: I say, How do you say this in English? Let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Could you repeat that, please? / How do you say ... in English? / What does ... mean?

Examples

  • Could you repeat that, please?
  • How do you say this word in English?
  • What does this word mean?

Execution method: Show the three phrases with a picture or a word. Ask guiding questions: Which phrase asks for the same information again? Which phrase asks for a new word? Then ask the student to use one phrase with a word they do not know.

Check question: Which phrase do you use when you want someone to say something again?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this short dialogue twice at a steady pace: Colleague: The report is in the folder. Student: Sorry, could you repeat that? Colleague: The report is in the folder. Student: What does folder mean? Colleague: It is the file.

Direct fact check

Ask: Where is the report? (in the folder). What did the student ask first? (could you repeat that). What does folder mean? (the file).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the student calm or worried? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows a polite request, such as Sorry, could you repeat that?

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Work word game: the tutor says a short sentence with one word the student may not know. The student asks for the word or for a repeat, then the tutor explains it simply. The student then uses the word in a sentence.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Could you repeat that, please?
  • How do you say ... in English?
  • What does ... mean?

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Do not translate for the student. Use simple words to explain, and praise each request for help.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Repeat please you.
  • What mean the word folder?

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
