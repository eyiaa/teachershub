Presentation Skills 2: Describing a Photo in Three Sentences

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a photo of a busy office. Ask: What can you see in this photo?

Tutor action: Point to one thing in the photo and give a short example: There is a computer. Let the student answer.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: There is + one thing / There are + many things / It is + adjective (big, busy, bright)

Examples

  • There is a desk near the window.
  • There are three people in the office.
  • It is busy and bright.

Execution method: Show the photo with labels. Ask guiding questions: Is it one thing or more? Which word do we use for the room? Then ask the student to make three sentences about the photo.

Check question: Do we say there is three people or there are three people?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this description twice at a steady pace: There is a large meeting room. There are four chairs around the table. It is quiet and clean.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is there in the room? (a large meeting room). How many chairs are there? (four). What is the room like? (quiet and clean).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the speaker like the room? Ask the student to point to the word that shows the feeling, such as clean.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Photo talk: the tutor shows a new photo of a workplace. The student describes three things in the photo using there is or there are, and one adjective with it is.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • There is a ...
  • There are ...
  • It is ...

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Let the student point and describe, and praise each complete sentence before correcting.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • There is two desks by the door.
  • It is a computer on the table.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
