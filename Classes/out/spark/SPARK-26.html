Directions and Services: Prepositions of Movement and Polite Requests

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: Where is the nearest supermarket to your home? Then show a town map and ask: How do you get to the library from the bus stop?

Tutor action: Accept an answer with gestures and simple words, such as left or straight. Then say the route back with imperatives, such as Go straight and turn left.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Imperatives have no subject: Go straight. Turn left at the bank. Cross the street. Walk along the river. Prepositions of movement: across, through, along, past, into, out of, towards. Polite requests: Could you tell me ...? / Would you help me, please? / Could I have ...?

Examples

  • Go straight and turn left at the bank.
  • Cross the street and walk past the café.
  • Walk along the river towards the park.
  • Excuse me, could you tell me how to get to the station, please?

Execution method: Show a town map with a bank, café, river, park, and station. Trace each route with a finger as you say it. Teach the prepositions of movement with gestures: through the park, across the street, along the river, past the café, into the shop, out of the station, towards the bridge. Contrast the imperative Turn left with the polite request Could you tell me ...? Ask the student to give a four-step route from the bus stop to the station.

Check question: Do we say Go to straight or Go straight?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this dialogue twice, slowly: Visitor: Excuse me, could you tell me how to get to the station? Local: Of course. Go straight, then turn right at the post office. Walk along the river towards the bridge. Cross the bridge and go past the café. The station is on your left, next to the park. Visitor: Thank you so much. Could you say that again, please? Local: Sure. Go straight, turn right, walk along the river, and cross the bridge.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the visitor want? (the station). Where do they turn right? (at the post office). What do they walk along? (the river). Where is the station? (on the left, next to the park).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the local helpful? Which words show it? (Of course, Sure).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Give directions from a station to a café in four steps, using imperatives and prepositions of movement on the map. The tutor follows the directions and asks Could you say that again, please? if needed. Then the tutor asks for a service, such as a table for two, and the student makes a polite request.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Go straight. Turn left / right. Cross ...
  • Walk along / past / through / towards ...
  • Could you tell me how to get to ...?
  • Could you say that again, please?
  • Could I have a table for two, please?

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use the same map for every task. Treat asking for repetition as good communication, not as a mistake. Mark the four-step directions on a 1 to 4 scale for clarity and accuracy.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Go to straight and turn left.
  • Cross the street and walk pass the café.
  • Could you to tell me the way?
