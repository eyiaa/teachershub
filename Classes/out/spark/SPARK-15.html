Everyday Communication: Invitations, Requests, and Social Plans

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What do you usually do on weekends? Do you like coffee shops? Show four activities: coffee, lunch, a movie, a walk in the park. Then ask: Would you like to get coffee this weekend?

Tutor action: Model the main phrase Would you like to ...? and tell the student it is more polite than Do you want to ...?

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Would you like to + verb? (an activity) / Would you like a / some + noun? (an offer) + Yes, I'd love to! / I'm sorry, I can't. I have to + verb. + Can you ...? / Could you ...? / Can I have ...? + How about ...? Let's meet at ... See you then!

Examples

  • Would you like to see a movie on Friday?
  • I'm sorry, I can't. I have to work.
  • Could you help me, please? Of course!
  • How about three o'clock? Let's meet at the café.

Execution method: Show the difference: Would you like to go ...? for an action, and Would you like a coffee? for a thing. Practise rising intonation for polite questions. The tutor invites the student to three activities, and the student accepts one and declines two with reasons (I have to study, I'm busy). Gap-fill requests: ___ I have a glass of water? Could you ___ me? (help or to help). Then suggest a time and place.

Check question: Do we say Would you like go or Would you like to go?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this dialogue twice, slowly: Kate: Hi, Omar! Would you like to have lunch on Saturday? Omar: I'm sorry, I can't. I have to work on Saturday. Kate: How about Sunday? Omar: Yes, I'd love to! Where? Kate: Let's meet at the Italian restaurant. Omar: Great. What time? Kate: How about one o'clock? Omar: Perfect. See you then!

Direct fact check

Ask: Why can't Omar have lunch on Saturday? (He has to work.) Which day do they choose? (Sunday). Where are they going to meet? (at the Italian restaurant). What time? (one o'clock).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is Omar polite when he says no? Which words show it? (I'm sorry, I can't.)

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Can you plan an outing? Scenario one: the student invites the tutor to an activity, suggests a time and place, and confirms the plan. Scenario two: the tutor invites the student, and the student declines politely, gives a reason, and suggests another day. Add one polite request in each scenario, such as Can you send me the address?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Would you like to ...?
  • Yes, I'd love to! / I'm sorry, I can't. I have to ...
  • Can you ...? / Could you ..., please?
  • How about ...? / Let's meet at ...
  • See you then!

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Let the student start each scenario. Check that the student uses to after would like, and note errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Would you like go to the cinema?
  • Sorry, I can't. I must to work.
  • Can you to help me, please?

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
