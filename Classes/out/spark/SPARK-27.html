Stories in Progress: Past Continuous with When and While

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What did you do last night? Then ask: What were you doing at eight o'clock last night? Tell me one thing you were doing.

Tutor action: Accept a simple answer. Say it back as a past continuous sentence, such as You were cooking at eight o'clock.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Was / were + verb-ing for a background action: I was cooking dinner. They were watching TV. Past simple for the interruption: When the phone rang, I was cooking. While I was walking, it started to rain. Negative: wasn't / weren't + verb-ing. Question: What were you doing?

Examples

  • I was cooking when the phone rang.
  • While I was walking, it started to rain.
  • She wasn't working when the lights went out.
  • What were you doing when the teacher came in?

Execution method: Draw a timeline with a long line for the background action and a short line for the interruption. Show four pictures: cooking when the phone rings, walking in the rain, sleeping when the lights go out, and reading when a friend arrives. Say the background action with was or were and -ing, then the interruption in the past simple. Show that when and while can start a sentence, with a comma after the first clause, or sit in the middle. The student makes two sentences from the pictures.

Check question: In the sentence I was cooking when the phone rang, which action is the background: I was cooking or the phone rang?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this story twice, slowly: Last night, Maria was cooking dinner when the power went out. She was surprised! While she was looking for a candle, her cat jumped on the table. Her friend Jon was walking to her house when he saw the lights go off in the whole street. He was worried, so he called her. When she answered, she was laughing.

Direct fact check

Ask: What was Maria doing when the power went out? (cooking dinner). What was she looking for? (a candle). What did her cat do? (jumped on the table). What was Jon doing when he saw the lights? (walking to her house).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: How did Maria feel at first, and how did she feel at the end? Which words show it? (surprised, laughing).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Tell an interrupted-event story: the tutor shows four pictures, of a person cooking, a person walking in the rain, a person sleeping, and a person studying. The student tells a four-sentence story with when and while, such as While I was studying, the phone rang. The tutor asks What happened next? after each sentence.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • I was ... when ...
  • While I was ..., ...
  • When ..., I was ...
  • What were you doing when ...?
  • Then, ... / After that, ...

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Give the student time to build the story, and do not interrupt them. Check whether the story has four connected sentences, and mark task completion and accuracy on a 1 to 4 scale.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I was cook when the phone rang.
  • While I walking, it rained.
  • I was watching TV when the phone ring.

Tutor checklist
