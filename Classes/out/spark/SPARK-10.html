Getting Around Town: Asking for and Giving Directions

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: Is there a supermarket near your home? What places are in your neighborhood? Then ask: How do you get to the supermarket?

Tutor action: Review the neighborhood words from the last class. Accept any answer, such as I walk or by bus, then move to the town map.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Excuse me, where is the ...? / How do I get to the ...? / Is there a ... near here? + Go straight. Turn left. Turn right. Stop. + It's next to / across from / between / near the ...

Examples

  • Excuse me, where is the bank?
  • Go straight and turn left. The bank is next to the supermarket.
  • The pharmacy is across from the park.
  • The library is between the bank and the school.

Execution method: Show a simple town map with a bank, hospital, supermarket, pharmacy, library, bus stop, school, and park. Use a hand gesture for each direction. Give one route and trace it on the map. Then say a route, and the student traces it. Keep instructions to two or three steps. Directions start with the verb, with no I or you.

Check question: Look at the map. Is the pharmacy across from the park or next to the park?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this dialogue twice, slowly: A: Excuse me, how do I get to the library? B: Go straight. Then turn left at the bank. The library is next to the school, across from the park. A: Thank you! B: You're welcome.

Direct fact check

Ask: Where does the man want to go? (the library). Where does he turn left? (at the bank). What is the library next to? (the school). What is across from it? (the park).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the man polite? Which words show it? (Excuse me, Thank you).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: I'm new in town: the tutor is a visitor at the bus stop. The visitor asks for three places on the map, and the student gives directions with two or three steps and one location phrase. Then switch: the tutor gives directions, and the student follows them on the map and names the place. If the student doesn't understand, they ask: Can you repeat that, please?

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Excuse me, where is the ...?
  • How do I get to the ...?
  • Go straight. Turn left. Turn right.
  • It's next to / across from / between ...
  • Can you repeat that, please?

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use the same map for every task. Treat asking for repetition as good communication, not as a mistake.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Excuse me, where is bank?
  • Go straight and turn in left.
  • The library is next the school.
