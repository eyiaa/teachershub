1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Ask: How are you today? What is one good thing that happened this week?

Tutor action: Give a short example: I am fine, thank you. I had a good weekend. Let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Greeting: Hello, how are you? / Answer: I am fine, thanks. / Turn the question back: And you? / Meeting someone: Nice to meet you.

Examples

  • Hi, how are you? I am fine, thanks. And you?
  • Nice to meet you. I am new here.
  • Did you have a good weekend? Yes, I did. What about you?

Execution method: Show a short dialogue with the phrases highlighted. Ask guiding questions: What do we say after the answer? Which phrase welcomes a new person? Then ask the student to practise one greeting and one question about the weekend.

Check question: After I am fine, thanks, what question do you ask back?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this short dialogue twice at a steady pace: Ana: Hi, how are you? Ben: I am fine, thanks. Did you have a good weekend? Ana: Yes, I went to the park. What about you? Ben: I stayed home and read a book.

Direct fact check

Ask: How is Ben? (fine). Where did Ana go? (the park). What did Ben do? (stayed home and read a book).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Are Ana and Ben friendly? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows interest, such as What about you?

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Coffee break: the tutor plays a colleague and starts with Hi, how are you? The student greets, answers, asks about the tutor's weekend, and makes one short comment about the day.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Hi, how are you?
  • I am fine, thanks. And you?
  • What about you?

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Act naturally, and praise each question the student asks back.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • How are you? I am good, and you how?
  • Nice to meet you, I am new in here.

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
