1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: What are you going to do tomorrow? Then ask: How are you feeling today? Show four faces: happy, tired, sick, relaxed. The student completes: Today, I feel ...

Tutor action: Link plans to feelings: What do you do when you feel tired? Students may not want to talk about their real health, so offer imaginary people and situations.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: I feel + adjective (sick, tired, dizzy) / I have a + problem (headache, cold, fever, stomachache, sore throat) / My + body part + hurts (one) or hurt (two or more) + You should / shouldn't + base verb + I need ... / Can I have ...?

Examples

  • I feel tired. I have a headache.
  • My back hurts. My feet hurt.
  • You should drink some water.
  • You shouldn't go to work today.

Execution method: Show a picture for each problem, and model the pronunciation of headache, stomachache, and sore throat. Point to body parts: head, eyes, ears, nose, mouth, throat, stomach, back, arms, legs, feet. Practise the three patterns: I ___ tired (feel or have). I ___ a headache. My stomach ___ (hurt or hurts). My feet ___. Then add needs and simple advice. Keep to everyday problems, not medical words.

Check question: Do we say I have tired or I feel tired? And My feet hurt or My feet hurts?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this dialogue twice, slowly: Ben: Hi, Ana. Are you okay? Ana: No, I'm not feeling well. I have a headache and a sore throat. Ben: Oh no! Do you have a fever? Ana: No, I don't. But I feel very tired. Ben: You should go home and rest. Do you need anything? Ana: Yes, I need some water, please. Ben: Here you are.

Direct fact check

Ask: What two problems does Ana have? (a headache and a sore throat). Does she have a fever? (No.) What does Ben suggest? (go home and rest). What does Ana need? (some water).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does Ben care about Ana? Which words show it? (Are you okay?, Oh no!, Do you need anything?).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: I don't feel well. Situation one: the student is a worker who feels sick, and the tutor plays the manager. The student says two symptoms and asks for help, such as going home or getting some water. Situation two: the tutor describes a problem. The student asks what's wrong, names the problem, says I'm sorry, and gives one suggestion with should.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • How are you feeling? / What's wrong?
  • I feel ... / I have a ... / My ... hurts.
  • I need ... / Can I have ..., please?
  • I'm sorry to hear that.
  • You should ... / You shouldn't ...

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use imaginary situations if the student prefers. This is everyday language, not medical advice. Note errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I have tired.
  • My head hurt.
  • You should to rest.
