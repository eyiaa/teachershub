Describing People and Places: Comparatives and Superlatives

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Review: Where do you live? Is it a city or a small town? Then ask: Which is bigger, your city or the capital? Show two city photos.

Tutor action: Start with adjectives the student already knows, such as big and small. Accept a short answer and repeat it back as a full comparison.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Short adjectives: adjective + -er + than (bigger than, cheaper than). Long adjectives: more + adjective + than (more expensive than). Irregular: good, better, the best; bad, worse, the worst. Superlative: the + adjective + -est (the biggest), or the most + adjective (the most popular). Use in or of: the best in town, the best of the three.

Examples

  • My city is quieter than the capital.
  • This restaurant is the cheapest in town.
  • The bus is more comfortable than the train.
  • Of the three cafés, this one is the best.

Execution method: Show two pictures: a big city and a small town. Write adjectives on the board in three columns: short (big, cheap, cold), long (expensive, comfortable, popular), and irregular (good, bad). Model big to bigger, and happy to happier. Ask the student to compare the two pictures with than. For a superlative, show a group of three and ask which is the most popular. Remind the student that -est and most never go together.

Check question: Do we say more cheap than or cheaper than?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this text twice, slowly: Ana and Tom live in different places. Ana lives in Lisbon. Lisbon is bigger than her old town, and the buses are more modern. Tom lives in a small village. It is quieter and cheaper than the city, but the shops are not as good. Tom says the people in his village are the friendliest in the region.

Direct fact check

Ask: Which place is bigger, Lisbon or Ana's old town? (Lisbon). Which place is quieter? (Tom's village). Which place is cheaper? (Tom's village). What does Tom say about the people? (They are the friendliest in the region).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Which person is proud of their home? Which words show it? (the friendliest people).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Which place is better for you? The tutor describes two places, such as two cafés or two towns. The student makes at least four comparisons with -er, more, and than, then chooses one place and gives a reason. Then the tutor names three places, and the student says which one is the best for a weekend.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • ... is bigger / cheaper / quieter than ...
  • ... is more expensive / modern than ...
  • This is the best / the cheapest ...
  • I prefer ... because it's ...
  • Which one is more ...?

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use places the student knows, if they prefer. Give the student time to build each comparison before you offer any help. Count the supported comparisons, target four, and mark task completion and accuracy on a 1 to 4 scale.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • My city is more quiet than yours.
  • This is the more cheap restaurant.
  • The café is good than the bar.
