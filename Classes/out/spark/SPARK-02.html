Hello! Nice to Meet You: Greetings and Introductions

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show four greetings: Hello! Hi! Good morning! Good afternoon! Say: Hello! Welcome! My name is Alex. Say hello to me.

Tutor action: Greet the student warmly and use facial expressions and gestures. Let the student repeat the greetings and say hello back. Check how comfortable the student is with basic English.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: I am (I'm) / You are (You're) / He is (He's) / She is (She's) + name or country. What's your name? My name is ... / Where are you from? I am from ...

Examples

  • I am Mario. I'm from Italy.
  • He is Peter. He's from Canada.
  • She is Elena. She's from Peru.
  • What's your name? My name is Sarah.

Execution method: Point to yourself, to the student, and to a photo to show I am, you are, he is, and she is. Show that What's means What is. Do this gap-fill orally: I ___ a student. She ___ from France. You ___ my friend. He ___ from Japan. Then the student makes two sentences about themselves. Show the pattern with examples, not grammar words.

Check question: Which word goes with she: am, is, or are?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this dialogue twice at a slow, steady pace: A: Hello! Good morning! B: Hi! Good morning! A: What's your name? B: My name is Maria. What's your name? A: I am Alex. Nice to meet you! B: Nice to meet you, too! Where are you from? A: I am from Canada. And you? B: I am from Mexico.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is the woman's name? (Maria). Where is Alex from? (Canada). Where is Maria from? (Mexico).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Are Alex and Maria friendly? Which words show it? (Good morning, Nice to meet you).

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Meeting someone new: you are at an online meetup. The student greets the tutor, says their name and country, asks the tutor's name and country, says Nice to meet you, and says goodbye. Do it twice: first the tutor starts, then the student starts. Finish with a challenge: the student does it once more with no help from the slide.

Target phrases and vocabulary

  • Hello! / Hi! / Good morning!
  • What's your name? My name is ...
  • Where are you from? I am from ...
  • Nice to meet you. Nice to meet you, too!
  • Goodbye! See you later! Have a nice day!

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Set the scene, then let the student lead. Do not interrupt. Recast gently by saying the correct sentence back, and note errors for the wrap-up.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I Maria. I from Mexico.
  • My name are Maria.
  • Where you are from?

Tutor checklist

☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?

☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?

☐ Am I speaking at a steady, clear pace without complex idioms?

☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
