Placement

Use this guide to place a new student in Spark, Flow, or Drive. Work through the three steps, score each pillar, then use the suggested level.

Student details

Learning goals (tick any that apply)

Step 1: Speaking and grammar test (10 to 12 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (1 to 2 minutes): a casual conversation about the student's job, daily routine, or learning goals.
  2. Three graded questions (8 to 10 minutes). Ask them in order, and listen for the grammar each one checks.
QuestionPromptWhat it checks
1. Present / daily routine"Tell me about your typical workday and main responsibilities."Present simple, daily vocabulary, subject-verb agreement
2. Past / storytelling"Describe a recent challenge at work or in life and how you handled it."Past simple, past continuous, connecting words (because, so, then)
3. Hypothetical / opinion"If you could change one thing about your career path, what would it be and why?"Conditionals, modal verbs, complex sentences

Step 2: Listening test (5 to 7 minutes)

Play a short 30-second audio clip, or use one of the five reading passages below. Ask one direct fact question and one question about underlying meaning.

Read one passage twice at normal speed, then ask its two questions in order. Use the answer guide to check the response, and accept reasonable paraphrases. Choose a passage close to the student's expected level, and move up or down if the first answers are weak.

Passage 1 · Level 1 (Spark)

My name is Ana, and I live in a small town near the coast. I work in a bank from Monday to Friday. In the morning, I drink coffee and walk to the office. In the evening, I cook dinner and call my mother.

  • Direct question: Where does Ana work, and on which days?
  • Meaning question: How does Ana feel about her daily life?

Look for: A bank, Monday to Friday. She seems content and settled, with a simple, comfortable routine.

Passage 2 · Level 1 to 2 (Spark to Flow)

Last summer, Tom and his brother went camping by a lake. On the first day, it rained, so they stayed in the tent and played cards. On the second day, the sun came out. They swam, cooked fish on a fire, and walked to the top of a hill.

  • Direct question: What did Tom and his brother do on the first day?
  • Meaning question: Why did they enjoy the second day more than the first?

Look for: They stayed in the tent and played cards. The weather improved, so they could swim, cook outside, and walk.

Passage 3 · Level 2 (Flow)

Maria was planning to move to Madrid for a new job. However, a week before she left, the company cancelled the position. She was disappointed at first, but she realised that she had more time to study, so she decided to stay and improve her English.

  • Direct question: What happened a week before Maria was due to leave?
  • Meaning question: Why did Maria decide to stay?

Look for: The company cancelled the job. She realised she had more time to study and improve her English.

Passage 4 · Level 2 to 3 (Flow to Drive)

Many people believe that cities should have more bicycle lanes. Cyclists are safer when they have their own space, and fewer cars mean cleaner air. Nevertheless, some shop owners worry that fewer parking spaces will reduce their customers.

  • Direct question: What do the shop owners worry about?
  • Meaning question: Does the speaker support the bicycle lanes? How can you tell?

Look for: They worry that fewer parking spaces will reduce customers. The speaker supports the lanes, shown by the points in favour and the word Nevertheless, which sets the concern aside.

Passage 5 · Level 3 (Drive)

The minister described the new housing plan as ambitious. Critics, however, pointed out that the plan had been announced three times before. One analyst remarked that the government seemed more interested in headlines than in homes.

  • Direct question: How many times had the plan been announced before?
  • Meaning question: What does the analyst suggest about the government?

Look for: Three times. The analyst is sceptical: the government cares more about publicity than about results, so ambitious is used with irony.

Placement rules for uneven profiles

Speaking and theory rule. Adult learners often understand more than they can say. Place the student where their speaking and grammar abilities meet.

Mixed skill rule. A student with Level 3 listening but Level 1 speaking and grammar goes to Level 2 (Flow). This builds their grammar base while keeping content engaging for their listening ability.

Spark (A1 to A2)

Foundations: basic sentence patterns and everyday topics (A1), then A2 bridge classes 17 to 31.

CEFR can-do summary

  • A1: Uses familiar everyday expressions and very simple phrases for concrete needs. Introduces themselves and others, and asks and answers questions about personal details. Communication works when the other person speaks slowly and clearly and is willing to help.
  • A2: Understands sentences and common expressions about areas of immediate relevance, such as family, shopping, and work. Handles short, routine exchanges on familiar matters. Describes background and everyday needs in simple terms.

Summarised in our own words from the Council of Europe Common Reference Levels. Read the full descriptors.

Open Spark classes

Typical student profile

  • New to English, or returning after a long break.
  • Speaks in short sentences and relies on set phrases.
  • Benefits from visuals, templates, and short, clear instructions.
  • Speaking confidence is the priority. Accuracy comes second.

Moving up

  • Scores Level 2 (Flow) on all three pillars at a re-test, using the placement indicators.
  • Places at Flow when speaking and theory meet at Level 2, under the speaking and theory rule.
  • Places at Flow with Level 3 listening and Level 1 speaking and grammar, under the mixed skill rule.

Common pitfalls

  • Correcting every error in the first sessions. Praise complete sentences first.
  • Talking for too long. Keep teacher talk under 40%, and use the visual templates.
  • Skipping the speaking block when time runs short. Speaking is the goal of this level.

Flow (B1)

Connected speech and steady conversation.

CEFR can-do summary

  • B1: Follows the main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters met at work, school, or in leisure. Deals with most situations that come up while travelling. Writes simple connected text on familiar or personal topics, and gives reasons for opinions and plans.

  • Explains opinions using simple connectors (because, but, so).
  • Knows the difference between Past Simple and Present Perfect.
  • Uses future forms (going to vs. will).
  • Uses basic modal verbs (can, should, must).

Open Flow classes

Typical student profile

  • Handles everyday topics, but struggles with long, unplanned speech.
  • Knows the core tenses and uses them mostly correctly.
  • Makes errors with perfect tenses, conditionals, and linking words.
  • Follows clear speech at medium speed, and misses some detail in fast speech.

Moving up

  • Scores Level 3 (Drive) on all three pillars at a re-test.
  • Completes the B1 readiness check (Flow class 40) at the standard required for Drive.
  • Places at Drive when the scores meet the Level 3 indicators on every pillar.

Common pitfalls

  • Lecturing on grammar instead of guiding the student to discover the rule.
  • Interrupting mid-sentence, which breaks the student's flow.
  • Saving error correction too long. Use the delayed feedback stage each session.

Drive (B2)

Style, tone, and debate.

CEFR can-do summary

  • B2: Understands the main ideas of complex text on concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussion in the student's field. Interacts with enough fluency for regular conversation with native speakers without strain. Produces clear, detailed text, and explains a viewpoint by weighing the advantages and disadvantages of options.

Summarised in our own words from the Council of Europe Common Reference Levels. Read the full descriptors.

ComprehensionConversationTheory

  • Understands complex arguments on familiar topics.
  • Picks up implied meaning and changes in tone.
  • Delivers clear, detailed presentations.
  • Participates actively in discussions and debates.
  • Explains viewpoints on issues, giving pros and cons.
  • Uses complex conditionals (2nd and 3rd).
  • Uses passive voice for formal reports.
  • Uses relative clauses and advanced linking words (however, consequently).

Open Drive classes

Typical student profile

  • Speaks fluently on familiar topics, and can argue a position.
  • Needs help with nuance, tone, idioms, and natural word choice.
  • Errors are mostly about precision and style, not basic grammar.
  • Often still translates ideas from their first language.

Moving up

  • Completes the B2 readiness check (Drive class 46) at the standard required.
  • Shows the Level 4 scores on speaking and grammar. The Level 4 path begins at the bridge classes (Drive 11 onward).

Common pitfalls

  • Staying in comfortable topics. Push the student toward debate and abstract argument.
  • Translating for the student. Ask them to rephrase in English instead.
  • Correcting style too early. Focus first on fluency and defending a view.

Placement at a glance

Use the Placement tab to score a new student. Use this guide to check your reasoning:

  • Speaking and grammar scores decide the level. Listening can place a student one level higher.
  • When listening is strong but speaking and grammar are weak, use the mixed skill rule.
  • Re-test after a few weeks before moving a student up. Do not move a student up after a single good session.
