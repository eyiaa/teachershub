Ordering Food: Would Like

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: GLOBALKEY-07   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Global Key (A2)

Target goal: Order food politely using would like and understand simple menu descriptions.

Can-do objective: Can order food, make polite requests, and understand a simple menu in a café or restaurant.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 10% | Theory 25-30% | Comprehension 25-30% | Conversation 25-30% | Wrap-up 5-10%

StageTimeShare
1. Warm-Up & Lead-In3 min10%
2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)8 min27%
3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)8 min27%
4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)8 min27%
5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up3 min10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a café menu with three drinks and three snacks. Ask: What would you like to order today?

Tutor action: Give a short example: I would like a coffee, please. Let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: I / We / He / She would like + noun (or to + verb), please.

Examples:

  • I would like a sandwich, please.
  • She would like a cup of tea.
  • We would like to pay now.

Execution method: Show the would like formula and the menu. Ask concept-checking questions: Is would like polite or rude? Do we use to before a verb? Then ask the student to order one item from the menu.

Check question: After would like, do we use a noun or a verb with to?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this dialogue twice at a steady pace: Waiter: What would you like? Customer: I would like the soup, please. Waiter: Would you like something to drink? Customer: Yes, I would like a glass of water.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the customer order first? (the soup). What does the customer order to drink? (a glass of water).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the customer polite? Accept a simple answer and ask the student to point to the phrase that shows politeness, such as please.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Café role-play: the student is a customer and orders two items from the screen menu from the tutor, who plays the waiter. Then they swap roles. The waiter asks: Would you like anything else?

Target phrases / vocab

  • I would like ..., please.
  • Could I have ...?
  • Yes, I would like ...

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use the menu on screen and praise each polite order before correcting anything.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I want a coffee, please, and I would like the cake.
  • I would like go to pay.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: I want a coffee, please, and I would like the cake.
    Native: I would like a coffee and the cake, please.
  • Accurate: I would like go to pay.
    Native: I would like to pay.

Checklist

  • ☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?
  • ☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did the lesson touch all three pillars?
  • ☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
