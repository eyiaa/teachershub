Shopping Basics: How Much Is It?

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: GLOBALKEY-01   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Global Key (A1)

Target goal: Ask how much things cost and understand simple price answers across all three pillars.

Can-do objective: Can ask about prices and understand simple numbers and short, direct instructions in a shop.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 10% | Theory 25-30% | Comprehension 25-30% | Conversation 25-30% | Wrap-up 5-10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a picture of a market stall with price tags. Ask: What is your favourite food to buy at a market?

Tutor action: Give a short example: I buy apples. Let the student answer, even with one word.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: How much is + singular noun? / How much are + plural noun? Answer: It is + price. / They are + price.

Examples:

  • How much is the bread? It is two euros.
  • How much are the apples? They are one euro fifty.
  • How much is the coffee? It is three dollars.

Execution method: Show the question formula with a singular and plural example. Ask concept-checking questions: Is bread singular or plural? Which verb do we use for apples? Then ask the student to ask about one item on the screen.

Check question: Do we say how much is the apples or how much are the apples?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this short dialogue twice at a steady pace: Customer: Excuse me, how much is the milk? Shop worker: It is one pound twenty. Customer: And how much are the eggs? Shop worker: They are two pounds.

Direct fact check

Ask: How much is the milk? (one pound twenty). How much are the eggs? (two pounds).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the customer polite or rude? Accept a simple answer and ask the student to point to the word that shows politeness, such as Excuse me.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Shop role-play: the student is a customer and asks the tutor, who plays the shop worker, about three items on the screen. Then they swap roles. The student must ask at least two price questions.

Target phrases / vocab

  • How much is ...?
  • How much are ...?
  • Thank you, that is fine.

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use the screen prices and praise each complete question before correcting anything.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • How much the bread is?
  • How much is the eggs?

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: How much the bread is?
    Native: How much is the bread?
  • Accurate: How much is the eggs?
    Native: How much are the eggs?

Checklist

  • ☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?
  • ☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did the lesson touch all three pillars?
  • ☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
