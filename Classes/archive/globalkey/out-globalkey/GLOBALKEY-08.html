Feeling Unwell: Should and Shouldn't

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: GLOBALKEY-08   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Global Key (A2)

Target goal: Describe a health problem and give simple advice using should and shouldn't.

Can-do objective: Can give and understand simple advice about health, and describe common symptoms and needs.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 10% | Theory 25-30% | Comprehension 25-30% | Conversation 25-30% | Wrap-up 5-10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Show a picture of a person with a headache. Ask: What should this person do?

Tutor action: Give a short example: They should rest. Let the student answer freely.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Subject + should / shouldn't + base verb (advice)

Examples:

  • You should drink more water when it is hot.
  • You shouldn't stay up late before a test.
  • She should see a doctor if the pain continues.

Execution method: Show the should and shouldn't formula. Ask concept-checking questions: Is it advice or a strong rule? Which verb form follows should? Then ask the student to give one piece of advice for the picture.

Check question: Do we say you should to drink or you should drink?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this short health text twice at a steady pace: If you have a cold, you should rest and drink warm drinks. You shouldn't go to work if you have a fever. If the cough lasts more than a week, you should see a doctor.

Direct fact check

Ask: What should you do with a cold? (rest and drink warm drinks). When shouldn't you go to work? (with a fever). When should you see a doctor? (if the cough lasts more than a week).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the text giving strict orders or friendly advice? Accept a simple answer and ask the student to point to the word that shows it, such as should.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Health advice: the student describes a common problem (a headache, a sore throat, tiredness) to the tutor, who asks what they should do. The student gives two pieces of advice to the tutor using should or shouldn't.

Target phrases / vocab

  • You should ...
  • You shouldn't ...
  • I think you should ...

Tutor mandate

Keep your talk time under 40 percent. Use the health picture on screen and praise each piece of advice before correcting.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • You should to rest.
  • You shouldn't to drink coffee at night.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: You should to rest.
    Native: You should rest.
  • Accurate: You shouldn't to drink coffee at night.
    Native: You shouldn't drink coffee at night.

Checklist

  • ☐ Are sentence structures clearly visible on screen?
  • ☐ Is Teacher Talk Time (TTT) under 40% of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did the lesson touch all three pillars?
  • ☐ Did the student speak using complete target sentences by the end?
