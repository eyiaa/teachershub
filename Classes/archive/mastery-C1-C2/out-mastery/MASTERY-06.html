Persuasive Devices: Rhetoric in Speech

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: MASTERY-06   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Mastery (C1)

Target goal: Use rhetorical devices such as rhetorical questions, repetition, and contrast to persuade an audience.

Can-do objective: Can produce clear, well-structured, detailed speech on complex subjects, using rhetorical devices to persuade.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 5% | Theory 10% | Comprehension 15-20% | Conversation 60-70% | Wrap-up 5-10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this topic: Should public transport be free? Ask for a one-minute opening opinion.

Tutor action: Let the student speak freely and note any rhetorical device they use naturally.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Rhetorical question + answer; repetition at the start of clauses (anaphora); contrast pairs (not X, but Y)

Examples:

  • Who really benefits from the status quo? Not the commuters.
  • We must invest in clean transport. We must invest in fair transport.
  • It is not about cost; it is about access.

Execution method: Show each device with an example and a label. Ask guiding questions: What effect does the repetition have? Who answers the rhetorical question? Let the student add one device to their warm-up opinion.

Check question: In a rhetorical question, does the speaker expect a real answer from the audience?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this speech extract twice at native speed: Why do we accept delays as normal? Why do we tolerate overcrowded carriages? It is not inevitable. It is a choice, and it is one we can change.

Direct fact check

Ask: What do we accept as normal? (delays). What does the speaker say about the choice? (it is one we can change).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker resigned or determined? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the attitude, such as we can change.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Public address: the student gives a three-minute persuasive speech on the warm-up topic to the tutor, who acts as an audience member. The student must use at least two rhetorical devices, and the tutor asks two questions from the audience afterwards.

Target phrases / vocab

  • Why do we ...?
  • It is not ... but ...
  • We must ..., we must ...

Tutor mandate

Guide a high-level discussion. Respond as a critical audience member, take silent notes on small errors, and keep the speaking share at 90 percent or more.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Why we accept the delays? It is normal.
  • It is not about money, is about people.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: Why we accept the delays? It is normal.
    Native: Why do we accept delays as if they were normal?
  • Accurate: It is not about money, is about people.
    Native: It is not about money; it is about people.

Checklist

  • ☐ Did the student speak for 90%+ of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did I take notes silently and hold corrections for the wrap-up?
  • ☐ Did the wrap-up separate accurate from native-like wording?
  • ☐ Did the discussion switch between formal and casual tone?
