Register Shifts: Formal and Casual in One Conversation

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: MASTERY-05   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Mastery (C1)

Target goal: Move between casual and formal register in the same conversation without losing clarity.

Can-do objective: Can switch smoothly between casual chat and formal meetings, adapting vocabulary and tone to the audience.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 5% | Theory 10% | Comprehension 15-20% | Conversation 60-70% | Wrap-up 5-10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this scenario: You meet a senior client at a casual lunch and the topic turns to a contract. Ask: How would you open the conversation?

Tutor action: Let the student open in whichever register they choose, then ask them to shift to the other register.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Casual: to be fair, honestly, I'd say. Formal: with respect, I would contend, it would seem that. Shift with a signal phrase.

Examples:

  • Honestly, I think the deadline is unrealistic. (casual)
  • With respect, I would contend that the deadline is unrealistic. (formal)
  • To be fair, the team did its best, so let us review the timeline. (a bridge between the two)

Execution method: Show the same message in casual and formal versions. Ask guiding questions: Who is the audience? Which words signal the register? Let the student deliver one message in both registers.

Check question: Which word signals a formal register: honestly, or with respect?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this short transcript twice at native speed: Look, I know it is a tight schedule. Still, with respect, the client expects a revised plan by Thursday, and I would contend that we can meet that if we prioritise the core features.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the client expect by Thursday? (a revised plan). What does the speaker think is needed? (prioritise core features).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Where does the speaker switch from casual to formal? Ask the student to point to the phrase that marks the shift, such as with respect.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Two-scene role-play: first the student and tutor chat casually about a weekend, then the conversation turns to a formal contract discussion. The student must make the shift at least twice and handle one interruption from the tutor in each register.

Target phrases / vocab

  • Honestly, ...
  • With respect, ...
  • To be fair, ...

Tutor mandate

Guide a high-level discussion. Switch registers yourself without warning, take silent notes, and keep the speaking share at 90 percent or more.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Hey, with respect, I would be honest with you.
  • Dear boss, yeah I think it is a bit too much.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: Hey, with respect, I would be honest with you.
    Native: Look, I would be candid with you, if I may.
  • Accurate: Dear boss, yeah I think it is a bit too much.
    Native: Dear Ms Lee, I feel the workload is rather too heavy at present.

Checklist

  • ☐ Did the student speak for 90%+ of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did I take notes silently and hold corrections for the wrap-up?
  • ☐ Did the wrap-up separate accurate from native-like wording?
  • ☐ Did the discussion switch between formal and casual tone?
