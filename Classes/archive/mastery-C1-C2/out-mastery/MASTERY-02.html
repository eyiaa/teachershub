Emphasis and Focus: Cleft Sentences and Inversion

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: MASTERY-02   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Mastery (C1)

Target goal: Use cleft sentences and inversion to emphasise key points in speech and writing.

Can-do objective: Can use advanced sentence structures for emphasis and organise information to highlight what matters most.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 5% | Theory 10% | Comprehension 15-20% | Conversation 60-70% | Wrap-up 5-10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this topic: What is the most underrated skill in the modern workplace? Ask for an opening answer in about 60 seconds.

Tutor action: Let the student speak freely. Note any structure they reach for to add emphasis.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: It is X that ... / What I find striking is ... / Rarely have I ... / Not only ... but also ...

Examples:

  • It is listening that most managers neglect.
  • What I find striking is how little attention we pay to tone.
  • Rarely have I seen a team recover so quickly from a setback.

Execution method: Show a plain sentence and its emphatic version. Ask guiding questions: What is being highlighted? What is the new sentence order? Let the student rewrite one of their warm-up sentences with emphasis.

Check question: In Rarely have I seen a team recover, why does the auxiliary verb come before the subject?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this column extract twice at native speed: Not only does the policy ignore regional differences, but it also assumes that every household can afford the upgrade. What the government calls reform is, in truth, a quiet transfer of cost.

Direct fact check

Ask: What does the policy ignore? (regional differences). What does it assume about households? (they can afford the upgrade).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: How does the writer feel about the government's word reform? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the attitude, such as in truth.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Persuasive talk: the student argues a position on the warm-up topic in a three-minute monologue, then defends it against two challenges from the tutor. Each main point must use an emphatic structure at least once.

Target phrases / vocab

  • What matters most is ...
  • Rarely does ...
  • It is ... that ...

Tutor mandate

Guide a high-level discussion. Let the student lead, take silent notes on small errors, and keep the speaking share at 90 percent or more.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • What I think is important is communication.
  • Rarely I have seen this happen.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: What I think is important is communication.
    Native: What matters most is communication.
  • Accurate: Rarely I have seen this happen.
    Native: Rarely have I seen this happen.

Checklist

  • ☐ Did the student speak for 90%+ of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did I take notes silently and hold corrections for the wrap-up?
  • ☐ Did the wrap-up separate accurate from native-like wording?
  • ☐ Did the discussion switch between formal and casual tone?
