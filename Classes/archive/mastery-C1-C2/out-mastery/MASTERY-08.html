Subtle Humour: Wit, Timing, and Self-Deprecation

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: MASTERY-08   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Mastery (C2)

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this line: I am not saying I am bad at planning, but my calendar has never once been ready for me. Ask: Why is this funny?

Tutor action: Let the student explain the humour, then ask them to add a line in the same style.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Understatement, self-deprecation, and callback (a joke that returns with a new twist)

Examples:

  • I am not saying I am disorganised, but my inbox has its own postcode.
  • I would love to help, but my schedule is currently a fiction.
  • As I said last week, the plan is perfect. The execution is a separate problem.

Execution method: Show each example and label the device. Ask guiding questions: What is the joke against? Who is the joke on? Let the student write one self-deprecating line about a small mistake they made recently.

Check question: In the inbox joke, is the speaker mocking someone else or themselves?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Play a short comedy sketch with fast native dialogue, or read this text twice at native speed: My cooking is famous in my flat. Unfortunately, it is famous mostly for the smoke alarm, which has now learned my face and says hello.

Direct fact check

Ask: What is the speaker famous for? (their cooking, or rather the smoke alarm). What has the alarm learned? (the speaker's face).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the speaker boasting or joking about their cooking? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the joke, such as famous mostly for the smoke alarm.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Witty exchange: the student and tutor trade light anecdotes about everyday failures, using self-deprecation and at least one callback to an earlier line. Keep the exchange natural and fast, and let the other speaker respond with a joke.

Target phrases / vocab

  • I am not saying ..., but ...
  • As I said earlier, ...
  • In my defence, ...

Tutor mandate

Guide a high-level discussion as a witty conversation partner. Match the student's pace, take silent notes on phrasing, and keep the speaking share at 90 percent or more.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • I am not saying I am bad, but I am bad.
  • My cooking is very funny because the smoke is making a noise.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: I am not saying I am bad, but I am bad.
    Native: I am not saying I am hopeless, but the evidence is against me.
  • Accurate: My cooking is very funny because the smoke is making a noise.
    Native: My cooking is legendary, mostly because of the smoke alarm.

Checklist

  • ☐ Did the student speak for 90%+ of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did I take notes silently and hold corrections for the wrap-up?
  • ☐ Did the wrap-up separate accurate from native-like wording?
  • ☐ Did the discussion switch between formal and casual tone?
