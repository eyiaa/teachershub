Measured Judgements: Hedging and Stance

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: MASTERY-01   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Mastery (C1)

Target goal: State a view with appropriate hedging and sustain it in an extended discussion.

Can-do objective: Can express complex ideas with precise, measured stance and qualify claims fluently and without strain.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 5% | Theory 10% | Comprehension 15-20% | Conversation 60-70% | Wrap-up 5-10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this topic: Is it fair to judge a society by its most successful citizens? Ask for an opening opinion in about 60 seconds.

Tutor action: Let the student speak without interruption. Note two stance phrases for the wrap-up.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: It could be argued that ... / To some extent, ... / There is a case for ... / Arguably, ...

Examples:

  • It could be argued that success reflects opportunity as much as effort.
  • To some extent, the criticism is justified, though it overlooks context.
  • Arguably, the problem lies less in the policy than in its execution.

Execution method: Show the hedges on screen with a strong claim and a hedged claim side by side. Ask guiding questions: What is the difference in certainty? When would a speaker choose each? Let the student restate their warm-up opinion with one hedge.

Check question: Which phrase shows the speaker is certain: arguably, or it is undeniable that?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this editorial extract twice at native speed: Few would dispute that meritocracy is an appealing ideal. Yet the evidence suggests that privilege, far from being incidental, shapes which talents are ever recognised in the first place.

Direct fact check

Ask: What do few people dispute? (meritocracy is an appealing ideal). What does the evidence suggest shapes recognition? (privilege).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: How does the writer signal the shift from the ideal to the critique? Ask the student to point to the word that marks the contrast, such as Yet.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Extended discussion: the student and the tutor debate the warm-up question for about twelve minutes. The student must use at least three hedges and must respond to two challenges from the tutor. Finish with a three-minute summary of the strongest point on each side.

Target phrases / vocab

  • It could be argued that ...
  • To some extent, ...
  • Arguably, ...

Tutor mandate

Guide a high-level discussion. Take notes on minor errors silently, let the student lead, and keep the speaking share at 90 percent or more.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • It is true that success is because of work.
  • Privilege is the reason for everything.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: It is true that success is because of work.
    Native: To a large extent, success reflects sustained effort.
  • Accurate: Privilege is the reason for everything.
    Native: Privilege plays a far larger role than is often acknowledged.

Checklist

  • ☐ Did the student speak for 90%+ of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did I take notes silently and hold corrections for the wrap-up?
  • ☐ Did the wrap-up separate accurate from native-like wording?
  • ☐ Did the discussion switch between formal and casual tone?
