Nuanced Counter-Argument: Conceding and Rebutting

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: MASTERY-09   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Mastery (C2)

Target goal: Concede a valid point in a debate and rebut the rest with precise, sophisticated language.

Can-do objective: Can express complex ideas precisely, concede points fairly, and rebut arguments in a sophisticated way.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 5% | Theory 10% | Comprehension 15-20% | Conversation 60-70% | Wrap-up 5-10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this claim: Economic growth always improves people's lives. Ask: Where do you stand on this, and why?

Tutor action: Let the student state a clear position in about 60 seconds, then challenge it with one counter-example.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: While it is true that X, the more pressing issue is Y. / I take the point, though I would question the premise that ...

Examples:

  • While it is true that growth creates jobs, the more pressing issue is who captures the gains.
  • I take the point about productivity, though I would question the premise that wages follow automatically.
  • That may hold in aggregate, but it obscures considerable variation between regions.

Execution method: Show a weak rebuttal and a refined one side by side. Ask guiding questions: What does the concession signal? What is the rebuttal targeting: the premise or the conclusion? Let the student rebut one counter-point from the warm-up.

Check question: Does the phrase I take the point signal agreement, disagreement, or a partial concession?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this editorial extract twice at native speed: Defenders of the scheme rightly point to the jobs it created. Their argument, however, rests on an assumption that the benefits were evenly shared, which the figures do not support.

Direct fact check

Ask: What do defenders point to? (the jobs it created). What assumption does their argument rest on? (benefits were evenly shared).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Does the writer accept the defenders' argument? Ask the student to point to the words that show the concession and the rebuttal, such as rightly and however.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Structured debate: the student takes a side on the warm-up claim and debates the tutor for twelve minutes. Each turn, the student must make one concession and one rebuttal, using precise language.

Target phrases / vocab

  • While it is true that ..., ...
  • I take the point, though ...
  • That may hold, but ...

Tutor mandate

Guide a high-level discussion as an intellectual equal. Take silent notes on phrasing, let the student lead, and keep the speaking share at 90 percent or more.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • Yes it is true but I don't agree at all.
  • Economy is good, but the people are not happy.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: Yes it is true but I don't agree at all.
    Native: While that is undeniably true, I would contest the conclusion.
  • Accurate: Economy is good, but the people are not happy.
    Native: Growth is encouraging, but its benefits are unevenly distributed.

Checklist

  • ☐ Did the student speak for 90%+ of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did I take notes silently and hold corrections for the wrap-up?
  • ☐ Did the wrap-up separate accurate from native-like wording?
  • ☐ Did the discussion switch between formal and casual tone?
