Industry Language: Business and Strategy Terms

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: MASTERY-03   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Mastery (C1)

Target goal: Discuss a business strategy using precise industry terms such as margin, stakeholder, and scalability.

Can-do objective: Can use professional and industry-specific terminology accurately in a formal discussion.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 5% | Theory 10% | Comprehension 15-20% | Conversation 60-70% | Wrap-up 5-10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this topic: What makes a start-up sustainable in the long run? Ask for an opening opinion in about 60 seconds.

Tutor action: Let the student speak freely and note any industry terms they use or avoid.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Term + collocation in context (profit margin, stakeholder buy-in, scalable model, cash flow pressure)

Examples:

  • The company is protecting its profit margin by renegotiating supplier contracts.
  • Getting stakeholder buy-in early makes the strategy easier to deliver.
  • The model is scalable only if customer acquisition costs fall.

Execution method: Show each term with a collocation and a plain-English equivalent. Ask guiding questions: Would a plain word be less precise here? Let the student replace two plain words in their warm-up answer with industry terms.

Check question: Which term refers to the people with an interest in a decision: stakeholder or shareholder?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this business news extract twice at native speed: The firm reported thinner margins this quarter, citing rising input costs. Analysts say that unless it can improve its scalability, investor confidence will remain fragile.

Direct fact check

Ask: What did the firm report? (thinner margins). What is the cause? (rising input costs). What must improve? (scalability).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Are the analysts optimistic or cautious? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the attitude, such as remain fragile.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Strategy presentation: the student presents a short business strategy to the tutor, acting as a board member, and defends it against two questions about cost and stakeholder support. The student must use at least four industry terms naturally.

Target phrases / vocab

  • The priority is to protect ...
  • We need stakeholder buy-in on ...
  • The model only scales if ...

Tutor mandate

Guide a high-level discussion. Act as a demanding board member, take silent notes on terminology, and keep the speaking share at 90 percent or more.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • The company has thin margins because it costs more.
  • We need the people who own shares to agree.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: The company has thin margins because it costs more.
    Native: The company's margins are under pressure from rising input costs.
  • Accurate: We need the people who own shares to agree.
    Native: We need shareholder buy-in before we proceed.

Checklist

  • ☐ Did the student speak for 90%+ of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did I take notes silently and hold corrections for the wrap-up?
  • ☐ Did the wrap-up separate accurate from native-like wording?
  • ☐ Did the discussion switch between formal and casual tone?
