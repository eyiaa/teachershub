Native Choice: Refining Fluent Speech

3-PILLAR CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK

Class: MASTERY-10   Student name: ____________________   CEFR phase: Mastery (C2)

Target goal: Speak fluently on a complex topic and refine word choices toward natural, native-like collocations.

Can-do objective: Can express complex ideas without hesitation, with native-like word choice, collocation, and natural flow.

Balance: Skeleton timing: Warm-up 5% | Theory 10% | Comprehension 15-20% | Conversation 60-70% | Wrap-up 5-10%

1. Warm-Up & Lead-In

Hook / prompt: Post this topic: Is originality overrated in art and business? Ask for an immediate, detailed opening opinion.

Tutor action: Let the student speak without interruption and note two collocations for the wrap-up.

2. Theory (Grammar & Mechanics)

Target structure / formula: Collocation and register: draw a conclusion, carry real weight, a sweeping generalisation, bear in mind, the bottom line

Examples:

  • We should be careful not to draw a sweeping conclusion from one case.
  • That point carries real weight, bearing in mind the evidence.
  • The bottom line is that originality depends on context.

Execution method: Show each collocation with a more textbook alternative. Ask guiding questions: Which version sounds more natural to a native speaker? Why? Let the student rebuild one of their warm-up sentences using a natural collocation.

Check question: Is it more natural to say draw a conclusion or make a conclusion in English?

3. Comprehension (Listening & Reading)

Material / context source: Read this essay extract twice at native speed: Originality is often treated as a virtue in itself. Yet most innovations are, on closer inspection, careful recombinations of existing ideas, and the bottom line is that context matters more than novelty.

Direct fact check

Ask: How is originality often treated? (as a virtue in itself). What do most innovations consist of? (careful recombinations of existing ideas).

Tone and implied meaning check

Ask: Is the writer dismissive of originality or measured about it? Ask the student to point to the phrase that shows the balance, such as on closer inspection.

4. Conversation (Speaking & Expressing)

Real-world task: Fluent debate: the student and tutor discuss the warm-up topic at native speed for twelve minutes, shifting between formal and casual tone at least twice. The tutor finishes with a challenge to the student's central claim.

Target phrases / vocab

  • The bottom line is that ...
  • That carries real weight, bearing in mind ...
  • I would be wary of a sweeping generalisation about ...

Tutor mandate

Guide a high-level discussion as a native-level coach. Take silent notes on word choice, let the student lead, and keep the speaking share at 90 percent or more.

5. Delayed Feedback & Wrap-Up

Self-correction prompts

  • We should not make a big conclusion from one case.
  • The most important thing at the end is that context is important.

Good vs. native-like upgrades

  • Accurate: We should not make a big conclusion from one case.
    Native: We should not draw a sweeping conclusion from a single case.
  • Accurate: The most important thing at the end is that context is important.
    Native: The bottom line is that context matters.

Checklist

  • ☐ Did the student speak for 90%+ of the lesson?
  • ☐ Did I take notes silently and hold corrections for the wrap-up?
  • ☐ Did the wrap-up separate accurate from native-like wording?
  • ☐ Did the discussion switch between formal and casual tone?
